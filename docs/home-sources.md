# Sources for the home page text

Each statement in the text section of the home page (`<section id="about-home">`, written by `site/home-text.mjs` and added only by the content-site build) comes from the place named here, read from this repository on 2026-10-05 and revised on 2026-10-06 after a review. A statement that cannot be traced is left out. When a feature changes, update the matching sentence. The wording is the section's own, but the facts are the About page's, so some short phrases are shared (city names, source names, "crust, mantle and core"). A review on 2026-10-06 found longer reused runs, measured that day: 22 words in the "Around you" sentence, 15 in the MIT licence sentence, 14 in the SGP4 sentence, 12 in the place search, 11 in the Data status sentence and 10 in the Tonight timeline; they were rewritten. `test/site.test.js` now fails if the section shares a run of 8 or more words with the About page or the count page, or repeats one of their h1 or h2 headings.

## The live figures strip (added 2026-10-06)
The values are not in the HTML. `site/home-strip.mjs` holds the functions; their own source text is copied into a small inline script (under 4 KB, no libraries, no `eval`, values set with `textContent`) that runs in the visitor's browser after the section. It reads `live/manifest.json` (never from the cache), then the five files the manifest names. Field names were read on 2026-10-06 from the collector's code (`pipeline/hazards.py`, `launches` and `nhc_storms`; `pipeline/feeds.py`) and from a real sample on the data branch (`git show origin/data:manifest.json` and the files it names, manifest `generatedAt` 2026-10-06T02:10:32Z). On that sample the functions gave 37 earthquakes, largest magnitude 5, Kp 4, 1 storm, 242,515 fire detections and the Nuri launch on 2026-10-07 03:23 UTC; `test/home-strip.test.js` checks the same functions against the live pages' own summaries on the saved real set of 2026-10-05.

| Figure | Definition | Same as |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Earthquakes in the last 24 hours | Events in `quakes.json` whose `time` is after `generated` minus 24 hours and not after `generated` | `summariseQuakes` in `site/hazard.mjs` (`count`), the earthquake page and `/right-now/` |
| Largest magnitude among them | The largest `mag` among those events, printed as the pages print numbers (`toLocaleString("en-GB")`); "none" when the window is empty | `summariseQuakes` (`largest.mag`) |
| Kp index now | The newest row of `kp.json` with a value whose time tag (UTC when it has no zone) is not after the visitor's clock | The app's `kpAt` (`src/info.js`); the aurora page's latest Kp (`summariseKp`, newest row with a value) is the same whenever no row is in the future, which was so in every sample read |
| Active tropical storms | The number of entries in `storms.json` `storms` (0 is a valid figure) | `summariseStorms` (`storms.length`), the storm page and `/right-now/` |
| Satellite fire detections in 24 hours | `detections` in `fires.json` (needs at least one cell) | `summariseFires` (`detections`) |
| Next rocket launch | The entry of `launches.json` with the earliest `net` after the manifest's `generatedAt`: its name and time in UTC, worded by the source's precision (SEC and MIN as a time, HR "to the hour", anything else, a month or quarter included, "not an exact date") | `src/launches.js` (`EXACT`, `whenText`); no live page shows launches |

Ages: each figure's data time is the quake list's `generated`, the Kp period's tag, the storm list's `generated`, the newest fire detection and the launch list's `generated`. When that time is older than its limit the figure stays and the line under the strip adds, calmly, "Some feeds are behind: ... (more than N hours old)". Limits: earthquakes 3 hours, Kp 8, storms 12 and fires 8 (`MAX_AGE_HOURS` in `site/hazard.mjs`, imported, so the live pages and the strip cannot disagree; the owner's message said 6 hours for fires, but `MAX_AGE_HOURS.fires` is 8 and the instruction was to mirror it), launches 6 (ours, `LAUNCH_MAX_AGE_HOURS`, the owner's value: launches have no live page and no limit of their own). The line also gives the manifest's `generatedAt` in UTC. A feed that is missing, broken or more than an hour in the future leaves its dashes; when the manifest cannot be read every dash stays and nothing is said.

## The share image (added 2026-10-06)
`site/assets/og-image.png`, 1200 by 630, about 220 KB, drawn by `tools/make-og-image.mjs` with the project's Playwright Chromium: a gradient background, a globe of circles and ellipses, three orbit ellipses with dots and faint dots for stars, in the CSS colours of `template.html`, the name and the tagline "A free live feed of what is above, around and under you" (the start of `APP_DESCRIPTION`). Everything is drawn from shapes in the script: no photo and no third-party artwork. The text uses the app's own fonts, Bricolage Grotesque and Hanken Grotesk, loaded from Google Fonts when the script runs; both are listed with `license: "OFL"` in their `METADATA.pb` files in the google/fonts repository (read 2026-10-06, `ofl/bricolagegrotesque` and `ofl/hankengrotesk`), and the SIL Open Font License allows using a font in an image. A system font was not used because some are licensed for their vendor's platforms only. The `og:image:alt` text describes the picture. Only fixed figures that describe how the app works: satellites launched in the last 30 days (SGP4), the 90-day calendar, places of about 15,000 people or more, the 10 degrees above the horizon that the tile counts from (`src/sky.js`), and the 24 hours in two of the strip's labels (the window of the earthquake and fire pages). ## Layout (for the owner to review)
- The section sits below the first screen, over the fixed app, as the design chose (`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-05-home-page-text-design.md`). The first screen is unchanged; `e2e-site.mjs` compares the stats strip, tab bar, canvas, top bar and layer chips with a build that has no section.
- "What is this? Read more" is a fixed link: hidden below 700px wide (phones reach the text from the About sheet's "What is this site? Read the overview"); from 700px to 899px at the right end of the place chip row, because the bottom of the screen there is the full-width tab bar with the layer chips above it; from 900px at the bottom left, level with the centred tab bar. Measured in Chromium on the built site on 2026-10-05: at 1280x800 the link box is x 16 to 195, y 743 to 779, the tab bar x 420 to 860, y 732 to 790, the layer chips x 608 to 1280, y 670 to 710; at 1440x900 the link is x 16 to 195, y 843 to 879, the tab bar x 500 to 940, the chips from x 768; at 768x1024 the link is x 573 to 752, y 62 to 98, beside the place and Tonight chips (which end near x 297). Widths depend on the font, so they move a little with the web fonts loaded (the tests block them).
- The link hides while the loader is still starting, a sheet or the search is open (CSS `:has`; a browser without `:has` simply keeps showing it). When the app cannot start it shows at every width, bottom left, and the scrollbar comes back (`scrollbar-width: thin`): WebGL failed (`src/main.js` replaces the loader's content with `#nogl`, so the loader's `.orb` is gone) or JavaScript is off (the noscript block's `div` is then parsed as an element; with scripting on it is plain text). The app sets no class when it starts, and adding one would need a `src/` change beyond `openAbout`, so the CSS reads the loader's own state instead. NOT CONFIRMED: a browser that hides scrollbars through `::-webkit-scrollbar` and does not support `scrollbar-width` (older Safari) keeps the scrollbar hidden in that state; the link still works.
- Keyboard: the top focus target (`#top`, tabindex -1) and the read-more link come just before the app, so Tab from the start of the page reaches the link first, and Enter scrolls to the section, which takes focus (tabindex -1). Focus moving into the app (Tab, Shift+Tab, a click) brings the first screen back, so focus is never left under the opaque text. "Back to the globe" scrolls to the top and returns focus to the top target.
- Printing shows the text only (`@media print` hides the app, the spacer and the link).
- The mouse wheel stays in the app on the first screen: a small inline script cancels a wheel on `#app` unless something inside it can still scroll that way, so the page reaches the text only by the links, the keyboard or a wheel over the text itself. With the page scrolled down, a wheel up over the app's controls scrolls back; over the globe the wheel zooms. Ctrl with the wheel (browser zoom) is never cancelled, and nothing is cancelled when 3D could not start.
