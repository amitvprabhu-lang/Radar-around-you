## Our own choices (OURS)

- Maximum ages: launches 6 hours, GDACS 6 hours, satellites 30 hours (the design's values); NHC's list is used for matching only when less than 12 hours old (the storm page's limit). A stale feed skips only its page (`site/build-live.mjs`); the previous copy stays.
- Guards: launches need a list with at least one launch, a readable time for each, dates within the collector's own window (3 days before to 800 days after the list's time, `pipeline/hazards.py`), and pad positions on the globe or none; GDACS needs a non-empty list, known types and alert levels, readable times, positions on the globe and a readable data time; the Starlink page uses the count page's plausibility bounds (5,000 to 60,000 active satellites) and is skipped (not failed) below 1,000 active Starlink satellites, a guard against a broken feed (11,149 in the bundled snapshot of 2026-10-04).
- The 30 day window of the launch page, the 15 launch table, the 10 km altitude bands, the whole-degree inclination groups, the 24 months of the launch chart, the five busiest launch days, the one degree coastline grid on these maps (half a degree on the other live pages), and the marker shapes and sizes of the disaster map (shape by type, size and colour by alert level, so colour is never the only cue).
- Findings ("What this means"): computed by `launchFindings`, `disasterFindings` and `starlinkFindings` in `site/events.mjs`; a change since the previous build uses `history.json` (`site/insight.mjs`, the last 48 builds of each page) and is left out when there is no earlier entry.
- The right-now hub's "What is notable" rules: a current GDACS event with an Orange or Red alert, a launch planned to the minute or second in the 24 hours after the list's time, an earthquake of magnitude 6 or more in 24 hours, a latest Kp of 5 (NOAA's G1) or more. These are checks, not warnings.
- JavaScript: progressive enhancement only (design section 8.1); every number is in the HTML. The live refresh reads the site's own `live/manifest.json` and feed files and updates only the numbers marked on the page (the next launch and the 30 day count; the current, Orange and Red counts); the Starlink page has no refresh because its data is binary.
