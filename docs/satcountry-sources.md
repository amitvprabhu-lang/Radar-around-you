# Sources for the satellites by country pages

What every figure on `/satellites-by-country/` and the five country pages rests on. Written on 2026-10-05 from the code and the bundled snapshot; nothing here was read from a new outside page. The feed, the definition of active, the orbit groups, the CelesTrak usage policy and the search notes are the same as for the satellite count page and are recorded in `docs/satcount-sources.md`; read that first. Anything marked NOT CONFIRMED has not been checked.

## The pages and why these five
- Design: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-05-satellites-by-country-design.md`. Code: `site/satcountry.mjs` (counting, positions, the guard), `site/svgmap.mjs` (the map), `site/pages-country.mjs` (the pages). Built on GitHub by `site/build-live.mjs` after each collection, and from the bundled snapshot by `site/build.mjs` at deploy time.
- The five owners are fixed in `COUNTRY_PAGES`. They were chosen from the bundled snapshot (`public/meta.json`, taken 2026-10-04 14:19 UTC), counted with `countOwners` on 2026-10-05: United States 12,530 active satellites, People's Republic of China 1,541, United Kingdom 697, Commonwealth of Independent States (former USSR) 381, Japan 134. The next owner is "To Be Determined" with 112 (not a place), then Italy 78. The snapshot has 99 owners with at least one active satellite and 16,629 active satellites in all; no active satellite in it lacks an owner.
- The guard (`MIN_ACTIVE_FOR_PAGE`, 50) is OURS: a check against a broken feed, not a content rule. A page whose owner is missing from the feed or has fewer than 50 active satellites is left out of that run, with a printed message, and the copy already on the site stays.

## What each figure rests on
- **Owner.** The catalogue's owner field. The collector (`pipeline/catalogue.py`, `pipeline/feeds.py`) reads each object's owner code from CelesTrak's SATCAT and turns the code into a name with CelesTrak's source code table, https://celestrak.org/satcat/sources.php (the page the collector downloads once a day; the pages cite it). `pipeline/pack.py` writes the names actually used into `meta.owners`. The pages show the names exactly as written there and never split, merge or rename an owner. The list mixes countries and organisations (the snapshot has, for example, "European Space Agency", "SES" and "To Be Determined"), and the former Soviet fleet is recorded as "Commonwealth of Independent States (former USSR)". The CIS page never uses another name for it, and a test checks that it never contains the word "Russia".
- **Active satellites per owner, share and rank.** `countOwners` uses the same rules as `countSatellites` (payload with status 1 to 5), and a test checks that the per-owner numbers add up to the count page's totals, on a fixture and on the bundled snapshot. Share is the owner's active satellites over all active satellites. Rank counts owners with at least one active satellite and leaves out "Not recorded".
- **Orbit table.** The owner's satellites in each orbit group against the share of all active satellites in that group, with the groups of `ORBIT_BOUNDS` in `site/satcount.mjs` (OUR working definitions, NOT CONFIRMED against a cited standard, as the count page says). The sentence above the table names the largest group and, when there is one, the group where the owner's share most exceeds the catalogue's; both are computed.
- **Purposes.** CelesTrak's own purpose groupings, as on the count page; the top eight are shown and the rest summed. When "Unspecified" is the largest, the page says how many have no grouping and names the most common recorded purpose.
- **Launch years and the last 30 days.** Launch dates from SATCAT. "Launched in the 30 days before the data time" is the collector's list `newIdx` (`pipeline/pack.py`: launch day on or after the reference date minus 30 days), counted for active satellites only, the same list the count page uses.
- **Starlink.** Satellites whose catalogue name contains STARLINK (`pipeline/pack.py`). In the snapshot every Starlink satellite is recorded under the United States (11,149); the hub's sentence about this is computed from the data, not typed.
- **Hub concentration.** The top owner's share, the top three's and the top ten's, and the number of owners with fewer than 10 active satellites, all computed. In the snapshot: 75.4, 88.8 and 94.3 percent, and 61 of 99 owners.

## The map
- **Positions.** For each active satellite of the owner, `ownerPositions` decodes its orbital elements with `decodeSwarm` and works out where it is with `swarmPositionEcef` and `ecefToGeodetic` (all in `src/core.js`): the two-body orbit with the main J2 drift that the live globe uses for its swarm, not SGP4. The point drawn is the point on the ground below the satellite.
- **Time.** Positions are for the data time (`meta.taken`), which the caption states. The design said "the build time"; the data time is used instead because it is the time every other number on the page refers to, it makes the page the same for the same data (so a rebuild without new data changes nothing), and the deploy-time copy built from the bundled snapshot would otherwise propagate old elements days or weeks forward.
- **Accuracy: NOT CONFIRMED and not stated.** The comment in `src/core.js` says the swarm model is good to tens to a few hundred kilometres over a day or two; that was not measured for these pages, and the collector accepts element sets up to 90 days old (`pipeline/pack.py`, `rejection`), so some positions can be further off. The page says the positions are approximate and gives no figure. The unit tests check only that positions are finite, on the globe, never further from the equator than the orbit's inclination allows, and on the equator for geostationary orbits.
- **Drawing.** Plain equirectangular projection. Points are rounded to 0.1 degree and points that round to the same place are one dot (the caption gives the number of dots when it differs); dots are drawn smaller for larger fleets. The busiest 30 degree band of latitude and the share north of the equator are computed from the unrounded positions.
- **Coastlines.** `public/coast.bin`, the app's own coastline file, made by `build_data.py` from `land-50m.json` (Natural Earth 1:50m outlines via the world-atlas package, as the code comments and the collector's static file note say). For the map they are merged onto a half degree grid. NOT CONFIRMED: the licence of the Natural Earth data and of the world-atlas package was not re-read for this record; check both before relying on it (Natural Earth's terms page and the world-atlas repository).

## What is NOT CONFIRMED
- Whether CelesTrak allows publishing counts derived from its data (its usage policy is silent; see `docs/satcount-sources.md`).
- That the owner field always means the satellite's operator or owning country in the way a reader expects. The pages say "as the catalogue records it" for this reason.
- The accuracy of the map positions (above) and the coastline licence (above).
- Whether these pages can rank or bring visits. The only evidence is a competitor's estimated traffic (Ubersuggest estimates supplied by the owner), recorded in the design, section 1.
