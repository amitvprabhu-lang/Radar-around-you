# Sources for the live hazard pages and the right-now hub

What every figure and typed statement on `/earthquakes-today/`, `/aurora-tonight/`, `/asteroid-close-approaches/`, `/tropical-storms-now/`, `/wildfires-today/` and `/right-now/` rests on. Written on 2026-10-06 from the code, the collector (`pipeline/config.py`, `pipeline/validate.py`, `pipeline/hazards.py`), the real collector output on the `data` branch (read with `git show origin/data:...`, collected 2026-10-05 about 18:40 UTC and saved as `test/fixtures/hazards/live-20261005/`), and the existing source records. Nothing here was read from a new outside page: every term below is quoted from a record already in this repository, and the records name when the page was read. Anything marked NOT CONFIRMED has not been checked. Design: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-06-live-hazard-pages-design.md`. Code: `site/hazard.mjs` (summaries, freshness, guards, the terms flags), `site/pages-hazard.mjs` (pages), `site/livepages.mjs` (the list of live pages), `site/build-live.mjs` and `site/build.mjs` (building).

## Our own choices (OURS)
- Maximum ages: quakes 3 hours, Kp 8, solar wind 6, storms 12, close approaches 48 (all the design's values); the aurora grid 6, fires 8 and GDACS events 12 (ours, reasons in the table). A stale feed skips only its page (`site/build-live.mjs`); the previous copy stays on the site.
- Guards: quakes need at least one event in the 7 day feed, magnitudes -2 to 10, depth -10 to 1000 km, positions on the globe; Kp 0 to 9 and at least one value; solar wind speed 100 to 3,000 km/s, density 0 to 500, Bt 0 to 200, Bz -200 to 200 (the collector's own ranges); the grid 65,160 values of 0 to 100; close approaches above 0 and at most 0.05 au (the query's own maximum), speed above 0 and under 100 km/s, H 0 to 40, a lunar distance of 300,000 to 500,000 km; storms wind 0 to 250 knots, pressure 800 to 1,050 mb; fires: the cell file must match `fires.json` (cell count, detections, counts by satellite) and have at least one cell. A guard failure skips the page with the reason and turns the workflow step red.
- The 24 hours of the quake and fire pages end at the feed's own time. Hourly quake columns are one-hour slices counted back from that time.
- The aurora grid threshold of 10, the map of the grid's equatorward edge, the one degree squares of the fire map (two degree squares above 8,000 squares, to keep the page small), and the nearest place limit of 300 km (the design's value).
- Object names: the collector (`pipeline/hazards.py`, `close_approaches`) strips brackets from both ends of JPL's full names, which leaves "524522 Zoozve (2002 VE68" with its bracket open. `pipeline/` is not changed for this; `objectName` in `site/hazard.mjs` adds a closing bracket only for an opening one that is never closed, and leaves names with no brackets or with balanced (also nested) brackets as they are.
- Close approaches: the page lists the passes from the start of the UTC day the list was read (earlier ones are counted and left out), and says that a pass earlier that day can already be over. A list with no pass in that window is refused by the guard (the previous page stays), because a 60 day window with none is not plausible (35 passes on 2026-10-05).

## Dataset markup
On `/earthquakes-today/`, `/tropical-storms-now/` (only when it prints no GDACS list) and `/wildfires-today/`. Not on the aurora page (Kp and the grid have no recorded terms), the asteroid page (no recorded terms), the hub, or the satellite pages. Properties: `name`, `description` (the page's lead as plain text, so every number in it is on the page; a test checks this), `url`, `creator` (the site), `isBasedOn` (the source's feed page), `dateModified` (the data time), `temporalCoverage` (the 24 hours to the data time, or for storms the earliest advisory to the data time), `spatialCoverage` ("Earth", or the NHC basins), `keywords`, `isAccessibleForFree`. No `license` (no licence URL is recorded for the derived summaries; a public-domain statement is not a licence URL) and no `distribution` (the pages offer no download). Whether search engines do anything with this markup for a page of summary numbers is NOT CONFIRMED.

- That 8 hours is the right limit for Kp: on 2026-10-05 the newest time tag was 15:00 UTC at a collection at 18:30. 8 hours leaves about 4.5 hours for NOAA to publish the next value; if the workflow log still shows the aurora page skipped, look at how late NOAA's values come before raising it again.

- The deploy-time copies: the bundled snapshot in `public/` has only `quakes.json` of these feeds, so a redeploy writes only the earthquake page and the hub; the other four hazard pages are absent until the next `pull.php` run copies them back (it copies every page whose file is missing or differs). Not tested on the host.

## Data times (and so `lastmod` and the IndexNow pings)
The data time is printed in each page's lead and meta line with what it is, and it is the page's `lastmod` in `sitemap-live.xml`. Rule: a page's bytes and its data time depend on the data, never on the minute our collector happened to read a feed, so a collection that brings nothing new changes nothing and pings nothing.
- Earthquakes: `generated`, the time USGS generated the feed ("the time given in the data itself").
- Aurora: the newest of the newest Kp row with a value, the solar wind's `updated` and the grid's observation time. Freshness of Kp is judged on the newest row with a value, so a newest row without one does not count as current.
- Close approaches: the start of the UTC day the list was read. Decided this way because the list's content does not depend on the read time within a day (the collector asks for "now" to 60 days, and the page lists from the day's start), so the page changes only when JPL's list or the day changes. Freshness (48 hours) is still judged on the read time.
- Storms: the newest `issued` or `updated` time among the active storms' advisories ("the time of the newest advisory in NHC's list"). With no active storm, the start of the UTC day the list was read, so an empty page changes at most once a day. Freshness (12 hours) is still judged on the read time. The GDACS list, when shown, carries no read time either.
- Fires: `newest`, the time of the newest detection in the files.
- The hub labels each row's time with what it is (satellite data, USGS feed, the Kp period's tag and the solar wind's time, the day the list was read, the newest advisory, the newest detection) and gives no structured date when no row has data.
- Tested: two builds from feeds that differ only in the collector's read time give byte-identical storm and asteroid pages, the same index entries and the same `lastmod` (`test/build-live.test.js`).

## Broken parts and failures
- A solar wind or aurora grid file that fails its checks (not merely old) leaves only its section out, with "not usable: it failed our checks"; the Kp page is still published and `build-live` prints a warning.
- A page that fails its guard or fails to render is skipped on its own, the run exits 1 after the other pages are written, and the message names the step and says whether a previous copy stays in the output folder.
- `llms.txt` always lists the five hazard pages and the hub with fixed names and notes, whether or not the deploy-time build wrote a copy (it writes only those whose data is bundled; the live copies arrive with the next pull).
- Removed as unsourced: "The cone of uncertainty is on each storm's NHC page" (`docs/hazard-sources.md` records the cone wording from NHC's KML, not where NHC shows it).
