# Sources and checks for /satellites-near-me/

The page lists the satellites whose ground point (the point on the Earth straight below the satellite, geodetic latitude and longitude) comes within a chosen ground distance (25, 50, 100, 250 or 500 km) of a place, right now and in the next 24 hours. Built on 2026-10-07 on the branch `feature/satellites-near-me`. Code: `site/near.mjs` (orbit model and search), `site/near-ui.mjs` (options, checks of outside values, wording, the map), `site/near-calc.mjs` (the browser calculation, run in a Web Worker), `site/near-page.mjs` (the page script), `site/pages-near.mjs` (the HTML), `site/near-summary.mjs` (build-time numbers), `site/near-site.mjs` and `site/near-assets.mjs` (build), `site/near-refresh.mjs` (deploy step), `site/near-errors.mjs` (the measured pass errors, generated), `tools/near-accuracy.mjs` and `tools/near-pass-errors.mjs` (the measurements). Tests: `test/near.test.js`, `test/near-accuracy.test.js`, `test/site.test.js` (build), `e2e-near.mjs` (`npm run e2e:near`). Anything marked NOT CONFIRMED was not checked.

## Data the page uses (no new data source)
- Only the site's own satellite files, same origin: `swarm.bin`, `ids.bin`, `details.bin`, `names.txt`, `precise.json` and `meta.json`/`satmeta.json`, from the live folder (`live/satellites/<version>/`, named in `live/manifest.json`) or, when that is missing or older, the copy bundled with the site (`public/`, copied to the site root). Layouts: `pipeline/pack.py`.
- Which copy: the app's rule, `resolveSources` in `src/live.js` (every file listed and the feed's `fetchedAt` newer than the bundled `meta.json`'s `taken`), in `chooseSource` (`site/near-ui.mjs`). A live feed past its own `staleAfterSec` but newer than the bundle is still used (it is still the newest data) and the page says so. The coordinator's request said "fall back ... when the live feed is missing or too old"; "too old" is taken to mean older than the bundled copy, because otherwise the fallback would be to older data. Reasons printed: no manifest, no satellites feed, files missing, older than the bundle, download failed.
- The page reads the manifest again on every calculation, and while it is open and visible looks at it at the manifest's `pollSec`, never under 300 s, doubling after each failure up to 30 minutes (`wPollDelay` of the embed widgets, `site/embed-models.mjs`). A new live version starts the same calculation again; the answer is redrawn in place (scroll position kept) and announced once as "Updated with orbit data from <time>, live feed. Done: ...". A live version whose files fail to download, or that does not decode (files that do not fit together), is remembered in the worker and on the page and never fetched again in that visit. The worker keeps the decoded data of the last good live version and goes on using it for every later calculation (a new place or distance included); the bundled copy is used only when no good live version was loaded. The page redraws and announces nothing for a broken version, and its wait before the next look grows with each failure and is reset only by an answer with new data (second review, 2026-10-07: before, the next change of distance fell back to the bundled copy and downloaded it again, and a version that did not decode was fetched on every look). After an error the old answer is removed, the error is said once, and the page tries again at the next look.
- Caches: the bundled files are fetched under the bundled data time (`swarm.bin?v=<taken>`) and `meta.json` under a unique address, so a cache that keeps old copies (the app's service worker `public/sw.js` refreshes bundled files in the background, and controls this page too) cannot mix two deploys' files; a mix that slips through is refused by `decodeFeed`'s length checks and ends in the error state (tested: a names file one line short gives the error, never shifted names). Two runs at once share one download.
- Places: the six cities of `snapshot.json`; the search uses the app's GeoNames list `public/places.json` (credit "Place names and time zones: GeoNames (geonames.org), CC BY 4.0", `docs/hazard-sources.md`), loaded only when the visitor types. The map's coastlines are the app's `public/coast.bin` (Natural Earth through world-atlas, public domain, `docs/embed-sources.md`).
- "Active satellites": payloads with status 1 to 5, the count page's definition (`ACTIVE_STATUSES`, `docs/satcount-sources.md`). Rocket bodies and debris are left out.
- Owner, purpose, launch date: SATCAT through `details.bin` and `meta.json`, printed "as the catalogue records it", linked to the five country pages (`COUNTRY_PAGES`). Orbit class: `orbitClass` and `ORBIT_CHART_LABELS` (`site/satcount.mjs`, OUR working definitions). Starlink flag: the swarm's kind 1 (`pipeline/pack.py`: the name contains STARLINK).
- Old data: an element set more than 72 hours old at the start of the calculation is left out and counted. 72 hours is the app's rule (`src/panels.js` tags a card "ORBIT DATA N DAYS OLD" over 72 hours; `pipeline/pack.py` counts sets over 3 days as stale). On the bundled data the count of satellites still usable falls from 16,517 at the data time to 15,466 two days later, 10,607 at 2.5 days, 1 at 3 days and 0 at 3.5 days (the reviewer's measurement, 2026-10-07). So the page warns at the top of its answer when more than 10% are left out for this (`WARN_STALE_SHARE`), and gives no answer, only a "too old" message, when fewer than 1,000 are usable (`MIN_USABLE`, OUR choice).
- CelesTrak's usage policy says GP data is updated every 2 hours (`docs/satcount-sources.md`; the live manifest's `refreshSec` is 7200). Its terms on republishing per-satellite results are silent (same caveat as the app and the count page): NOT CONFIRMED.

## The orbit model, and why
- The app's globe uses a fast two-body model with J2 drift (`swarmPositionEcef` in `src/core.js`); its comment says it is good to "tens to a few hundred kilometres over a day or two". The page needs a 25 to 100 km answer, so this was measured first (below). Result: 62 km at the 95th percentile one day after the data time, too coarse.
- Decision: the page uses SGP4 from satellite.js 7.1.0, the library the app already bundles for its pass predictions (`src/sgp4.js`); licence MIT (read from `node_modules/satellite.js/package.json`, field `license`, 2026-10-07). Satellites listed in `precise.json` (stations, the brightest objects and everything launched in the last 30 days; 187 in the bundled copy) get their full element sets. Every other satellite gets SGP4 from the mean elements packed in `swarm.bin` (`swarmOmm` in `site/near.mjs`, the inverse of `pipeline/pack.py`); `swarm.bin` carries no BSTAR or mean motion derivatives, so drag is zero. No data source was added and the collector is unchanged.
- What SGP4 from the packed elements fixes: the app model's short-period error of about 8 km at the data time (median 7.85 km, against 0.43 km). What it cannot fix: drag on low satellites, because the drag terms are not published for them. Adding `BSTAR` and `MEAN_MOTION_DOT` to the published swarm (a collector change, about 8 bytes per object) would remove most of the error below 600 km; not done here (the task allowed no new server files); this is the owner's choice.
- Search (`closestApproaches`): for each satellite, the times when the place is within the search angle of the orbit plane are found in closed form (the place's height above the plane is a sine of time); inside those windows the search steps with skips no longer than the angle can close at the fastest possible angular rate (perigee rate plus the Earth's rotation, 10% spare), steps inside the zone (at most a 500th of an orbit, up to 2 minutes for slow orbits) and keeps every local minimum of the sampled angle there, then fits parabolas to the squared angle and to the squared ground distance. A minimum at the edge of the 24 hours is not a pass. Geostationary satellites (in the geostationary belt and inclined under 2 degrees, `GEO_MAX_INCL_DEG`, OUR choice) are sampled every 10 minutes and listed apart; inclined geosynchronous satellites (87 over 5 degrees in the bundled data: QZSS, BeiDou IGSO, IRNSS and others) go through the pass search. The first version put them all in the stationary list and missed, for example, QZS-4 passing 22 km from Sydney at 25 km and BeiDou-2 IGSO-2 8 km from Singapore (found by the review; now tested). Checked in `test/near.test.js` against an equatorial orbit with a known answer (every pass 55.6 km from a place at 0.5 degrees north, one per lap) and against brute force (exact distance every 10 s, 12 hours, about 30 real element sets and 3 places at 500 km): every brute-force minimum was found within 20 s.
- Distance: `haversineKm` (`src/core.js`, sphere of 6,371 km) between the place and the geodetic ground point from `ecefToGeodetic`. Elevation and azimuth: `ecefLook`; sunlit: `isSunlit` with `sunUnitVectorEci` (the app's cylindrical shadow). Visibility to the eye is not claimed.

## Accuracy: the measurement (2026-10-07)
- Data: CelesTrak `https://celestrak.org/NORAD/elements/gp.php?GROUP=active&FORMAT=json`, downloaded once on 2026-10-07 at about 04:38 UTC (7,034,238 bytes; 16,689 sets after the collector's own rejection rules). Not committed; a stratified sample of 841 sets is committed as `test/fixtures/near-accuracy.json` (columns as `precise.json`), with the band counts of the whole download.
- Method (`tools/near-accuracy.mjs`): ground distance between the ground point from full SGP4 (with drag terms) and (a) the page's model, (b) the app's swarm model, both from the same set packed exactly as `pipeline/pack.py` packs it (checked against the published `swarm.bin` for 20 rows of `public/precise.json`).
- From the data time (every set at its real age; median element age 10.1 h, 95th percentile 24.9 h), km, median / 95th percentile / maximum:

| Model | +0 h | +6 h | +24 h |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| App's swarm model | 7.85 / 14.4 / 1,308 | 7.87 / 18.9 / 1,003 | 9.06 / 62.0 / 4,989 |
| Page's model (SGP4, packed elements, no drag) | 0.43 / 7.4 / 851 | 0.69 / 15.0 / 1,014 | 2.44 / 59.6 / 4,996 |
| Page's model, satellites from 450 km up | | | 1.94 / 26.7 / 3,176 |

- This table (`POSITION_P95` in `site/near.mjs`) is the error of the whole position at a moment, almost all of it along the track. It explains the choice of model. It is NOT the error of a pass and is not used to mark passes: the first version did use it, which made the page's "±" tens to thousands of km, about 99% of its borderline rows false alarms, and dropped every pass below 450 km (found by the review of 2026-10-07).

## Accuracy of a pass (what the page prints)
- An error along the track moves when a pass happens much more than how close it comes. So the page states two measured uncertainties per pass: of the closest ground distance (km) and of the time of closest approach (s).
- Method (`tools/near-pass-errors.mjs`, second version after the second review of 2026-10-07): the page's model run on older published data against SGP4 from the newer CelesTrak sets (the download above). Older data: three collections of 2026-10-05, at 08:14:54 (the repository's `live/satellites/20261005T081455Z/`), 14:00:36 and 22:01:07 UTC (copies of the data branch the reviewer kept; folders with `swarm.bin`, `ids.bin`, `details.bin`, `precise.json`, `satmeta.json`). 12 places (Pune, Tromsø, Svalbard, Fiji at the date line, the Aleutians at the date line, Quito, 0/0, McMurdo, London, New York, Tokyo, Sydney), five windows of 12 hours from 12 to 72 hours after each older data time. Each truth pass within 300 km is matched with the model's nearest pass in time (within 15 minutes): 1,166,209 passes of 16,689 satellites. Command: `node tools/near-pass-errors.mjs <gp.json> <download time> <older folder> [<older folder> ...] --write` (about 5 minutes on this Mac). Only the resulting table (`site/near-errors.mjs`) and a small sample (`test/fixtures/near-accuracy.json`, the 08:14 collection only) are committed.
- Kinds, measured apart (site/near.mjs `passKind`): "station", a full element set of a crewed station, recognised by the swarm's display kind 4, which `pipeline/pack.py` gives to the stations it lists (ISS 25544 and Tiangong 48274; an explicit list in the collector, tested); "fullNew", a full set of a satellite launched in the last 30 days (most still raising their orbits); "full", any other full set; "mean", the swarm's compact elements without drag. The first version pooled the stations with about 100 new launches below 450 km, which gave the ISS 50 to 118 km and 4 to 8 minutes where it is good to about half a km and a few seconds (the review measured 0.25 to 0.55 km and at most 3.2 s for the two stations), so it was never listed at 25 or 50 km.
- Rules for small samples (printed by the tool for every bucket they touch): a bucket of fewer than 300 passes takes the value of the bucket before it (the first one the row's all-ages value); a value may rise above the bucket before only when 500 or more passes say so; a row of fewer than 100 passes is left out (the kind's "other" row, or the compact data's, is used); a row of fewer than 300 passes in all gets a floor of 1 km and 10 s. Values are rounded up to 0.5 km and 5 s, at least 0.5 km and 5 s, and never fall with age. The first version's 72-hours-and-more column came from a few hundred passes and jumped to 79.5 km (600 to 1,000 km) and 188 km (1,000 to 2,000 km); with the three data sets those buckets have 21,432 and 16,998 passes and are 7 km and 0.5 km.
- The table (km, s) by hours since the element set at the pass, under 36 / 36 to 48 / 48 to 60 / 60 to 72 / 72 and more:
  - compact, 450 to 600 km: 1.5, 5 / 2, 10 / 2, 10 / 2.5, 10 / 2.5, 15 (passes 121,821 to 234,766 per bucket)
  - compact, 600 to 1,000 km: 0.5, 5 / 0.5, 5 / 1, 5 / 1, 5 / 7, 50
  - compact, 1,000 to 2,000 km: 0.5, 5 in every bucket
  - compact, below 450 km: 9.5, 60 / 13.5, 80 / 22, 160 / 26.5, 175 / 26.5, 175
  - compact, medium orbits 0.5, 5; inclined geosynchronous 2.5, 515 (163 passes, all ages); high elliptical 29, 360 (242 passes, all ages)
  - station: 1, 10 (122 passes: the small-row floor; the passes themselves gave 0.5 km and 2.8 s)
  - launched in the last 30 days, below 450 km: 24, 110 / 68.5, 305 / 118.5, 540 / 172.5, 610 / 183, 755; 450 to 600 km 3.5, 10 then 105, 465 at 72 hours and more; 600 to 1,000 km 6, 40 then 66.5, 360; 1,000 to 2,000 km 159, 825 (all ages)
  - other full sets: below 450 km 5, 40 and 450 to 600 km 2, 20 (all ages); others 0.5, 5 rising to 9.5, 50
- How the page uses it: "within" when the central estimate is inside the distance; "borderline" when outside by less than the distance uncertainty; counted but not listed only when that uncertainty is as large as the distance. A full set that is not a station, below 450 km or launched in the last 30 days, never gets less than the compact figure for its band. A time uncertainty from 3 minutes up is written "time uncertain by about N min". For "right now" the uncertainty is the distance uncertainty plus the ground covered in the time uncertainty. When more than a quarter of the usable satellites will be in the 72-hours-and-more bucket before the 24 hours end, the page says so (`WARN_AGING_SHARE`).
- Marks checked out of sample (`--marks`): the same three older data sets, windows 12 to 24, 36 to 48 and 60 to 72 hours, at 20 other places (high latitudes, the equator, mid latitudes; `CHECK_PLACES`, none used for the table). Percentages:

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 25 km | 79,824 | 92.9 | 93.9 | 97.5 | 9.1 | 88.3 | 95.3 | 95.9 |
| 100 km | 287,388 | 98.1 | 98.6 | 98.8 | 10.8 | 88.5 | 94.3 | 95.5 |

  At 25 km, 3.9% of the true passes were counted as too uncertain (compact data below 450 km, whose distance uncertainty reaches 26.5 km) and 2.2% had no model pass within 15 minutes. The reviewer's independent figures for the version before (28 places, three data sets): "within" rows truly within 97.0, 98.9 and 99.8%, borderline truly inside about 9.5, 11 and 10%, coverage about 95%.
- Weak groups in this check (few passes, honest limits): stations 6, 23 and 135 listed rows, all inside their "±"; inclined geosynchronous satellites at 500 km 71% inside the distance "±" (372 rows); new launches at 1,000 to 2,000 km 38% and high elliptical new launches 22% (659 and 175 rows: satellites changing orbit quickly). - Limits, said on the page: one day of older data (three collections) against one newer download two days later, one week of October 2026; the newer sets are fitted predictions, not true positions; manoeuvring satellites can be further off. Repeat with the tool whenever newer pairs exist; NOT CONFIRMED that the figures hold at other times.
- `test/near-accuracy.test.js` measures the committed sample (841 stratified sets plus the 184 that had full sets in the 08:14 collection, with that collection) and fails if the compact data's 95th percentiles under 72 h exceed 3 km and 20 s (450 to 600 km and 600 to 1,000 km) or 40 km and 240 s (below 450 km). It also checks that the page's table still covers the sample, labelled as a regression guard: the sample is part of the data the table was made from. The out-of-sample check is the table above (the older data of 14:00 and 22:01 could not be committed as a small fixture without their whole files).
- The second check (real world): the published live data of 2026-10-05 08:14:54 UTC (its `swarm.bin`, packed elements, no drag) propagated to the epochs of the 2026-10-07 element sets, against SGP4 of those newer sets at their own epoch. 36 to 48 hours ahead: all satellites median 9.5 km, 95th percentile 127 km (10,456 sets); 450 to 600 km 12.2 and 48 km; 600 to 1,000 km 0.8 and 18 km; below 450 km 57 and 1,335 km. This includes manoeuvres and drag changes, so it is larger than the model error; the page says so and calls "give or take" the least the position can be off by. Not repeated at other times; the sample's old rows are in the fixture.
- `test/near-accuracy.test.js` fails if, on the committed sample (catalogue-weighted), the page model's 95th percentile exceeds 10 km at +0 h, 20 km at +6 h, 70 km at +24 h, or 30 km at +24 h for satellites from 450 km up; or if any band's sample 95th percentile exceeds the table by more than 50% plus 1 km. On the sample: 6.5, 13.3, 58.9 and 22.2 km.
- NOT CONFIRMED: SGP4's own error against the true position (the repository's earlier notes give "about 1 km at epoch, 1 to 3 km a day" only from Wikipedia, `docs/feature-sources.md`); whether one download is typical; the sample's 45 high elliptical sets make that band's figures rough.

## Numbers on the page
- Expected at once: active satellites times the share of a sphere inside a cap of the chosen radius, `(1 - cos(r/R))/2` with R = 6,371 km; 100 km gives 31,415 km² of 510.06 million km², 6.16e-5. Computed at build time from the data the page is built with (the bundled copy in `npm run site`; the downloaded live feed in `npm run build:hosting` via `site/near-refresh.mjs`), and again in the browser with the data used. The page says satellites are not spread evenly, so this is an order of magnitude.
- The example (Pune, 100 km): worked out at build time for the 24 hours after the data time, never after the build time, and labelled with its data time and source.
- "A straight line of 100 km never reaches a satellite": the lowest perigee among active satellites in the data the page is built with (135 km in the bundled copy of 2026-10-04) and the share above 300 km, both computed.
- The site's six cities and their time zones: `snapshot.json`.

## Privacy and storage
- The device's position is asked only on the button, rounded to 0.01 degree, never stored, never put in the address, never sent (the calculation runs in the browser; the only requests are to the site's own files).
- A place chosen from the list, the search or typed coordinates, with the distance, goes into the address (`?lat=&lon=&r=&name=&tz=`) and `localStorage` (key `radar-near-place`, inside try/catch). The default place is written to neither. Every value from the address or storage is checked (`parseQuery`, `restoreStored`): numbers in range with at most 6 decimals, a name of 1 to 60 characters without control characters or angle brackets, a time zone that `Intl` accepts, a radius from the list; names are written with `textContent` only.

## Performance (macOS, this Mac, shared with other jobs; load averages noted)
- Node, the bundled data (16,514 satellites worked out), one calculation of 24 hours, load 17 to 29: Pune 25 km 568 ms, 100 km 430 ms, 500 km 1,310 ms; London (near the Starlink inclination, longer windows) 100 km 848 ms; preparing the satellites (SGP4 set-up) 176 ms once per data and 10 minutes. Earlier runs at load 3 to 4: 330 to 700 ms for 100 km.
- Chromium (Playwright, `e2e-near.mjs`), in the worker, a new distance with the data loaded: 393 to 976 ms over five runs at load averages 4 to 65 (657 and 693 ms in the final run at load about 24); the first answer including the download 2.7 to 5.6 s from a local server. The main thread had no long task over 50 ms during worker calculations; with Chrome's 4x CPU slowdown the longest was 0 to 125 ms.
- Chrome's CPU slowdown does not slow a worker's thread, so the calculation itself was timed on the main thread (no worker, sliced in 12 ms parts) with the 4x slowdown: 4.1 to 9.3 s for 50 km. Its longest task was 230 and 288 ms in two runs before the last fixes (formatters made once, the result and the map in tasks of their own) and 150 and 116 ms after them (the test's bound is 200 ms). So the target of about 1.5 s on a mid-range phone is NOT met by this measure: expect a few seconds on such a phone, with the page responsive throughout (worker and progress bar). Real phones: NOT CONFIRMED.
- After the review round (a472e36, load about 12): first answer 1.9 s, a new distance 263 and 355 ms in the worker, no main-thread task over 50 ms; with the 4x slowdown and no worker 3.5 s for 50 km and a longest task of 89 ms (the test's bound for this fallback is now 300 ms, from the spread measured above).
- Sizes: page HTML about 33 KB; `near-page.<hash>.js` about 25 KB and `near-calc.<hash>.js` about 37 KB (minified, before compression); the data the calculation downloads about 0.9 MB raw from the live folder (swarm 386 KB, names 280 KB, details 155 KB, ids 77 KB, precise 25 KB, satmeta 6 KB), about 0.34 MB with Brotli by the bundled copy's own size table (`public/manifest.json`). The place list (2.0 MB raw, about 0.9 MB compressed) only when the visitor searches, and the coastlines (84 KB) for the map.

The widest figures (up to 183 km, 755 s) apply to new launches still raising their orbits, not to the stations, which are good to about half a km.
