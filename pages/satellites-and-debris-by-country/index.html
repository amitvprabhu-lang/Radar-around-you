What this means

Every object in the public catalogue carries an owner code, which CelesTrak's source table names as a country or an organisation. The three largest owners, the United States, the CIS (former USSR) and China, hold 88.9 percent of everything in Earth orbit. Debris carries an owner code too. In this data, 93.4 percent of the 12,525 pieces of debris in Earth orbit carry the owner of a satellite from the same launch; 826 do not. The catalogue does not record who or what broke a piece off, and these pages do not say.

The catalogue is not everything up there. NASA's Orbital Debris Program Office says "Large orbital debris (> 10 cm) is tracked routinely by the U.S. Space Surveillance Network"; smaller fragments are estimated by sampling and are not listed one by one. 801 of the objects counted here have no public orbit data in the catalogue.

The last 12 months

In the 365 days to 10 October 2026, 1,407 catalogued objects re-entered the atmosphere, most of them recorded under the United States with 779, China with 313 and the CIS (former USSR) with 133. 4,850 objects launched in those days are still in orbit, most under the United States with 3,704, China with 611 and To Be Determined with 114. 35,686 objects in the catalogue have re-entered since 1957. 302 objects of 12 owners are not counted here because the catalogue records them around the Moon, the Sun or another body.

How these numbers are made

  • Source: CelesTrak's whole satellite catalogue (SATCAT), which our collector reads once a day as one file, and CelesTrak's active list of orbit data, read about every 2 hours for the satellite count page.
  • In Earth orbit: no decay date in the catalogue, and an orbit centre of Earth or a docking to another object (station modules and visiting craft). On 10 October 2026 this gave 34,982 objects.
  • An active satellite is a payload with the status Operational, Partially operational, Backup, Spare or Extended mission; every other payload is inactive. This is our definition, the same as on the satellite count page.
  • Rocket bodies, debris and unknown objects are the catalogue's own object types. The owner is the catalogue's owner code, named with CelesTrak's source table and never renamed or merged; the list mixes countries and organisations, so an object recorded under an organisation is not counted under any country.
  • The orbit groups on the owner pages use the perigee and apogee in the catalogue: eccentricity 0.25 or more is high elliptical, otherwise the mean of the two heights sets the group (our working definitions, as on the satellite count page).
  • Owner pages exist for owners that had at least 30 objects in Earth orbit when the pages were planned (not "To Be Determined"); a page is left as it is while its owner has fewer than 20.
  • The pages are rebuilt when either source has a new version. A copy whose catalogue is more than 72 hours old is not rebuilt.

Questions

How many satellites are in orbit?

CelesTrak's catalogue of 10 October 2026, 08:25 UTC lists 20,102 satellites in Earth orbit: 17,198 active and 2,904 inactive. The orbit data of 11 October 2026, 02:30 UTC has 16,689 active satellites with current orbits, the number on the satellite count page.

How much space debris is there?

12,525 catalogued pieces of debris in Earth orbit, plus 2,300 spent rocket bodies, as of 10 October 2026, 08:25 UTC. Pieces too small to track are not in the catalogue, so the real number of fragments is far higher.

Which country has the most space debris?

China, with 4,225 catalogued pieces in Earth orbit (33.7 percent), then the United States with 3,895 and the CIS (former USSR) with 3,893, as the catalogue records owners.

Does the owner of a piece of debris say who made it?

No. The catalogue gives each piece an owner code and does not record how the piece came about. In this data, 93.4 percent of the 12,525 pieces of debris in Earth orbit carry the owner of a satellite from the same launch; 826 do not.
