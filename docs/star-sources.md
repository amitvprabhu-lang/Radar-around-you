# Sources for launches, star details and planets

Everything here was read on 2026-10-05 from the page named, or measured from the source's own response. Anything marked NOT CONFIRMED could not be checked from a primary page. Re-read each page before a public launch: terms and URLs change.

## The Space Devs, Launch Library 2 (rocket launches)
- Feed: `ll.thespacedevs.com/2.3.0/launches/upcoming/?limit=30&mode=normal&hide_recent_previous=true`. No key was needed. `mode=normal` is needed because the shorter list mode was not checked for pad positions.
- Terms, from The Space Devs' own FAQ (`github.com/TheSpaceDevs/Tutorials`, file `faqs/faq_TSD.md`): "You are free to use the data in any way, shape, or form, and share what you create with it. Please refrain from forwarding it without adding value." Accuracy of the information is not guaranteed because of the amount of manual work. "Attribution is not mandatory, but is encouraged and appreciated." We credit them on the Launches screen and in About.
- Rate limit: the same FAQ says Launch Library 2 free access is "limited to 15 calls per hour", and that they "heavily" encourage caching the output and not letting user clients query the API directly. The API's own `api-throttle` endpoint answered `your_request_limit: 15, limit_frequency_secs: 3600` on 2026-10-05. The collector asks once an hour (a quarter of the allowance) and stops at the first refusal (halt group `ll2`). NOT CONFIRMED: whether many GitHub runners sharing addresses could use up the allowance; the halt rule is the guard.
- How exact a time is: each launch carries `net_precision`. The API's own descriptions, from the real answer: SEC, MIN and HR mean the planned time is accurate to the second, minute or hour; M "The T-0 is expected in the given month."; Q4 "The T-0 is expected in the fourth quarter of the given year." Of 30 launches on 2026-10-05, 7 had a firm time and 23 only a month or quarter, with the date set to the last day of the month. The app shows a month or quarter as vague and never gives it a clock time or a countdown. NOT CONFIRMED: the full list of precision codes (only SEC, MIN, HR, M and Q4 appeared); any other code is shown with the source's own name for it.
- Status words are the source's own: "Go for Launch" (Current T-0 confirmed by official or reliable sources), "To Be Confirmed" (Awaiting official confirmation), "To Be Determined" (Current date is a placeholder or rough estimation based on unreliable or interpreted sources).
- Images: the API returns image links, and the pad image in the answer carried the licence "CC BY-NC 2.0". No images are used.
- Not read: the full API documentation page (it is drawn by script and returned only its title).

## HYG database v4.4 (star distances, spectral types, luminosities)
- Source: `codeberg.org/astronexus/hyg`, file `data/hyg/CURRENT/hyg_v44.csv.gz` (13.6 MB, served by Git LFS; 119,614 stars; the GitHub copy is archived and points to Codeberg). The CSV header: id, hip, hd, hr, gl, bf, proper, ra, dec, dist, pmra, pmdec, rv, mag, absmag, spect, ci, x, y, z, vx, vy, vz, rarad, decrad, pmrarad, pmdecrad, bayer, flam, con, comp, comp_primary, base, lum, var, var_min, var_max.
- Licence, from the repository README: "This work is licensed under a Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 International License." Versions since 4.0 use it. Because it is share-alike, `raw3/hyg_v44_extract.csv` and `public/stardetails.json` are adaptations and are offered under the same licence (see `raw3/HYG-NOTICE.txt`). The rest of the project's code stays MIT. This is the call I made for you; the other choice was the Hipparcos data, whose licence is non-commercial.
- Column meanings, from `data/hyg/README.md`: `dist` is in parsecs, "A value >= 100000 indicates missing or dubious (e.g., negative) parallax data in Hipparcos"; `lum` is "Star's luminosity as a multiple of Solar luminosity"; `absmag` is the apparent magnitude from 10 parsecs. One parsec is 3.262 light-years by the README's rounding; the app uses 3.2616 (648000/pi astronomical units over 63241.077).
- Trap found: HYG gives star 207 a distance of 100000 and a luminosity of about 39 million Suns. The luminosity is worked out from the bogus distance. The builder drops luminosity and absolute magnitude whenever the distance is not usable (test `no luminosity ... without a usable distance`).
- Cross-checks run, all passing: 5,041 of the app's 5,044 catalogue stars are found by Hipparcos number (the other three, 55203, 78727 and 115125, are not in HYG and get no details); the largest position difference is 0.005 degrees and the largest magnitude difference 0.04; 102 stars have no usable distance; absolute magnitude agrees with apparent magnitude and distance to within 0.1 for all stars that have a distance (`test/starinfo.test.js`); HYG has no repeated Hipparcos number in the rows used.
- Not claimed: how accurate the distances are. They are Hipparcos parallax values, rounded for display (one decimal under 10 light-years, whole numbers to 100, two significant figures above).

## NASA Exoplanet Archive (stars with confirmed planets)
- Service: the Table Access Protocol endpoint `exoplanetarchive.ipac.caltech.edu/TAP/sync`, table `pscomppars` (Planetary Systems Composite Parameters). The query used is in `raw3/exoplanet-hosts.README.txt`. No key was needed; 1,164 planet rows came back with a Hipparcos name, and 1,163 of them had a name in the form the builder reads (the other is skipped).
- Column meanings, from the Archive's column list: `hip_name` "Name of the star as given by the Hipparcos Catalog"; `sy_pnum` "Number of confirmed planets in the planetary system".
- Acknowledgement the Archive asks for (its acknowledgement page): "This research has made use of the NASA Exoplanet Archive, which is operated by the California Institute of Technology, under contract with the National Aeronautics and Space Administration under the Exoplanet Exploration Program." It is shown in About and on the star index page. The page also asks users of a specific literature reference to acknowledge it; we use none directly. NOT CONFIRMED: a data licence statement; none appeared in the text read.
- Cadence: the Archive's TAP pages state no update interval, so the planet list is a snapshot taken 2026-10-05 and is rebuilt by hand with `python3 -m pipeline.stardetails`. It is not a live feed.
- Duplicates handled: names such as "HIP 28393 A" and "HIP 28393 B" are one Hipparcos entry; a planet listed twice counts once; the count shown is the number of distinct planet names, not `sy_pnum`, because for 9 `hip_name` values in the file `sy_pnum` differs from the number of rows (the Archive counts planets of the whole system, which can include another component).
- Cross-check: wherever both the Archive (`sy_dist`) and HYG give a distance for a planet-hosting star in the catalogue, they agree to within 15 percent; all 103 hosts agree.

## Duplicates between sources (all tested)
- The same storm from NHC and GDACS: matched by name, then by position, in one place (`src/dedupe.js`). The GDACS copy of an NHC storm is dropped on the globe, in Around you and in the storms list; GDACS cyclones NHC does not list stay. The old rule in Around you dropped every GDACS cyclone whenever NHC listed any storm.
- Launches: one per id (the later update wins), and one per name and planned time; the count dropped is published as `duplicatesDropped`.
- Stars: one HYG row per Hipparcos number; planet hosts merged by Hipparcos number.
- Already in place before: satellite element sets (duplicates dropped, counted in Data health), GDACS events (one per event id), places (a GeoNames place within 30 km of one of the six cities is skipped).
- Not merged on purpose: fire detections. The three FIRMS files come from three satellites on different passes; the same fire seen on two passes is two observations, and the Fires screen says detections can count a fire twice.

## Sky Lens (camera behind the sky view)
- No data source. It uses the browser camera and orientation interfaces. The browser tests start Chromium with a fake camera, so what is tested is the real code path, not a real phone.
- NOT CONFIRMED on a real device: how well phone compasses line the stars up with the picture, the camera's field of view (the start value of 60 degrees is our guess; the person pinches to match), and whether iPhone browsers allow the camera and motion permissions together as the code expects.
