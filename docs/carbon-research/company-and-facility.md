## 2. Source sections

### 2.1 Climate TRACE (emissions and ownership)

- Owner: Climate TRACE coalition (secretariat WattTime). Ownership data powered by coalition member Global Energy Monitor.
- URLs read (all 2026-10-06): https://climatetrace.org/terms ; https://climatetrace.org/data ; https://climatetrace.org/faqs ; https://climatetrace.org/news/understanding-and-using-climate-trace-ownership-data-for-emitting-assets (dated Apr 20, 2026) ; https://climatetrace.org/news/how-to-parse-asset-ownership-in-climate-trace-data (Feb 25, 2023) ; https://climatetrace.org/news/tracing-ghg-emissions-to-specific-sources (Nov 13, 2022) ; data guide PDF "Data Licensing, Schema, and Citation Guide", Version 5.11.0, https://media.climatetrace.org/about_the_data_latest_b6e7b8d419.pdf ; API reference https://api.climatetrace.org/v7/docs/index.html and its OpenAPI file https://api.climatetrace.org/v7/docs/openapi.json (version 7.2.0) ; live calls to https://api.climatetrace.org/v7/... .
- What is measured: modelled estimates, "Modeled using data from third-party sources that are either publicly available or available through licensing agreements, or Collected from publicly available sources" (terms page). Facility (asset) level, 2.77 million sources summarised from 744.7 million assets. Mostly scope 1: "Climate TRACE primarily focuses on emissions that can be physically observed in the real world, i.e. Scope 1 emissions (i.e. direct emissions occurring at a source). However, we do provide Scope 2 emissions ... for some sectors such as heavy industries. These are included in the 'other' data columns. Further, while Climate TRACE does not provide Scope 3 emissions for downstream industries, data users can use the source-level emissions data to calculate their own Scope 3 supply chain emissions." (FAQ)
- Years and cadence: API says "Annual data is available for 2021 through the current year" for sources, monthly from January 2021. Latest release 5.11.0 on Sep 24, 2026 with "monthly emissions data through July 2026" (about 2 month lag). Releases appear monthly (5.10.0 in August 2026 through June 2026).
- Ownership completeness (article of Apr 20, 2026): "Climate TRACE currently includes ownership information for 18 emissions-intensive subsectors. Together, this represents full ownership data for assets responsible for 32% of global emissions, plus immediate owner data for another 14% of global emissions." My arithmetic: roughly 54% of global emissions have no owner data. Counts: "With parent entity data (ultimate owners): ~14,000 unique owners, ~26,000 assets covered. With immediate owner data (direct owners): ~13,800 unique owners, ~26,000 assets covered." Sectors with high coverage (80 percent or more of sectoral emissions) are in an interactive chart whose list I could not read: NOT CONFIRMED. The FAQ ("What ownership data is available for [sector]?") lists asset-level ownership "either partially or fully" for: aluminum, bauxite mining, coal mining, copper mining, cement, domestic and international aviation, electricity generation, enteric fermentation and manure management (cattle operations), iron mining, iron and steel, oil and gas production, oil and gas transport, oil and gas refining, petrochemicals steam cracking, pulp and paper. FAQ also says: "We have tried our best to provide ownership data where available but in many cases it is not available/not updated."
- How ownership works: "Immediate owner: This is the direct owner, and sometimes operator, of the asset. Ultimate owner: This is the highest-level parent entity (or entities) of the asset, often a holding company, investment firm, or government body." "Each ownership link records the percentage owned, including both controlling and minority stakes (as long as they exceed a 5% threshold)." CSV files are "a top-down summary linking large parent companies to the assets they control ... This is done via shortest, but not all, paths." The full network (nodes and edges) is available "by contacting us directly".
- Responsibility caveat from Climate TRACE itself (Nov 2022 article): "Should the emissions be assigned to the immediate owner? To a parent company if it's wholly owned? To equity investors? To the offtaker of the power, whether a utility or another entity? ... we leave it for users to decide how to incorporate such data into their own analyses."
- Dashboard vs CSV (Feb 2023 article): the dashboard showed one owner (Petoro) for Troll, while the CSV showed five (Petoro 56 percent, Equinor, Shell, TotalEnergies, ConocoPhillips). The website shows a primary owner only.
- CSV ownership file fields (data guide, `<subsector>_emissions_sources_ownership.csv`): parent_name, parent_entity_id, parent_entity_type, parent_lei ("GLEIF Legal Entity Identifier"), parent_permid, parent_registration_country, parent_headquarter_country, overall_share_percent ("Share percent from multiplying all the share percentages in the ownership_path together"), ownership_path ("Shortest ownership path between the entity and the asset"), ownership_path_datasource_ids, immediate_source_owner, immediate_source_owner_entity_id, source_operator, source_operator_id, percentage_of_operation, source_id. The guide says the emissions CSV also has per-gas confidence classes ("very high", "high", "medium", "low", "very low").
- Ownership data sources (terms page): "Facility ownership information has been made available from a variety of sources, including primary sources such as company websites, secondary sources such as industry news articles, and aggregators such as PermID, OpenCorporates, and Wikipedia."
- API (tested live today): GET https://api.climatetrace.org/v7/... returned HTTP 200 with no key, header access-control-allow-origin: *. Endpoints: /v7/sources (ranked sources, filter by year, gas, sectors, subsectors, gadmId, cityId, countryGroup, continent, ownerIds, limit, offset), /v7/sources/{id} (monthly time series, `owners` [{id, name}], per-year `confidence`), /v7/sources/emissions (aggregate by sector, supports ownerIds), /v7/owners?name= (search by name), /v7/rankings/countries, /v7/definitions/*, /v7/admins, /v7/cities. Owner fields in the API are only id and name (no ownership percentage). Gases: co2, ch4, n2o, co2e_100yr, co2e_20yr. Observed quirks: some assets return `owners: null`; Shell Pernis Refinery returned "Shell PLC" twice in `owners`; capacity/activity/emission-factor fields show "license restricted" for some sources. The owner search returns many legal entities with similar names (for example Shell PLC, Shell UK Ltd, Shell Deutschland GmbH). Rate limits: none published. Page says: "As a beta release, we cannot guarantee availability of the Climate TRACE API; please keep volume low and use it with caution in production settings." Also public BigQuery dataset trace-data-383422.climate_trace (billed to the user; free tier 1 TiB per month).
- Attribution observation: querying /v7/sources with ownerIds=Shell PLC (E100000000746) and with ownerIds=Equinor ASA (E100000000766) both returned "Brazil_Santos_Deepwater" with the same 27,621,187 t CO2e (2024). So the by-owner source list shows whole-asset emissions, not stake-weighted. Whether /v7/sources/emissions weights by ownership share: NOT CONFIRMED. (That endpoint gave 71,497,272 t CO2e100 for Shell PLC in 2024; I cannot say whether that is equity-weighted.)
- Licence (terms page, quoted): "The emissions data and associated metadata has been made available via Climate TRACE under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International License (CC BY 4.0), with the exception of external datasets listed below. In general, this means that anyone is free to copy, modify and distribute Climate TRACE data in any format for any purpose, including commercial use, as long as you attribute it to Climate TRACE and indicate if you have made any changes." FAQ: "all users are free to copy, modify and distribute Climate TRACE data in any format for any purpose including commercial use, as long as you acknowledge Climate TRACE."
- External data inside the Climate TRACE set has separate terms: EDGAR and FAOSTAT country-level estimates; source-level estimates for some "Other manufacturing" sources from E-PRTR, US EPA FLIGHT, Israel PRTR; some "Solid Waste Disposal" sources from FLIGHT, EPA LMOP, Canada GHGRP. "It is the sole responsibility of the user to review the terms and conditions for all the above sources".
- Citation (data guide): "Climate TRACE (2026), Climate TRACE Emissions Inventory v5.11.0, https://climatetrace.org [Date Accessed]." Ownership: "Mowat, A., Louis, G., and Bird, M. (2025). Climate TRACE Ownership Information: Global Energy Monitor Methodology for Ownership Data. Global Energy Monitor, USA, Climate TRACE Emissions Inventory. https://climatetrace.org [Accessed date]".
- Redistribution: allowed (CC BY). Trademark: "Climate TRACE® and the Climate TRACE logo are registered trademarks of WattTime Corporation" (terms), so do not use the logo to imply endorsement.
- Limitations in their own words: "The Climate TRACE coalition has no control over any aspects of such data, including its accuracy, timeliness, completeness or consistency." "The information is provided 'as is' and 'as available'". Accuracy FAQ: confidence "varies, and is generally lower in fields where no highly certain methods have yet been found". For disputed plants: "Climate TRACE methods have varying levels of uncertainty. You can see from the 'confidence' value ..." and a feedback link on each source card. Fair-naming risk: estimates by asset, ownership partial, shortest-path ownership, owner shares mostly in CSV not API.

### 2.2 Carbon Majors (InfluenceMap and Climate Accountability Institute)

- URLs: https://carbonmajors.org/Downloads ; https://carbonmajors.org/ ; https://carbonmajors.org/Methodology ; https://carbonmajors.org/FAQ ; https://influencemap.org/terms (all read 2026-10-06).
- Measured: historical entity-level emissions from fossil fuel and cement production. "Carbon Majors is a database of historical production data from 178 of the world's largest oil, gas, coal, and cement producers ... 100 Investor-owned Companies, 72 State-owned Companies, 6 Nation States". Data spans from 1854, "over 1.44 trillion tonnes of CO2e covering 70% of global fossil fuel and cement emissions since ... 1751". Unit: entity (company or state), not facility. Estimated from self-reported production with emission factors.
- Scopes (Downloads page): "Scope 1 emissions - four direct operational emissions sources. Note these are only partial scope 1 emissions, only emissions that are production-linked." "Scope 3 emissions - emissions from the combustion of marketed products. Note these are partial scope 3 emissions categorized as Scope 3: Category 11 'use of sold products', which has been modified to quantify emissions from each entity's net production as opposed to sold products." FAQ: "around 88%" is scope 3 category 11.
- Years and cadence: latest "21/01/2026: Release of the updated Carbon Majors dataset, includes data from reporting year 2024." Earlier: 05/03/2025 (RY2023), 04/04/2024 (first release at carbonmajors.org). The page says "The data is updated annually in November" but actual releases were March 2025 and January 2026 (page inconsistent with its own log).
- Access: three CSV granularities (low: year, entity, entity type, LEI, total emissions; medium: adds commodity and production; high: adds operational emissions, reporting entity, data source), plus data dictionary and README. No API seen. Includes LEIs "where available, a parent or primary LEI is provided".
- Licence, exact: Downloads page: "The Carbon Majors dataset is available for download and for non-commercial use, subject to InfluenceMap's Terms and Conditions." Terms 1.2: "You may not engage in the use of the Site or any of its content, information or data: For any commercial purposes whatsoever, including (but not limited to): a) In a tradable index or investment product; b) In a subscription or fee-based database service and c) To be republished, packaged, or resold for commercial purposes." The Terms say the "Site" includes carbonmajors.org. Terms 2.2: "We grant to you a personal, revocable, non-exclusive, non-transferable licence to use this Site for non-commercial purposes only ... provided that wherever you cite the data, you credit 'InfluenceMap.org' as the source". Also forbids to "reproduce, duplicate, copy, modify, adapt, translate, create derivative works of ... republish ... 'scrape,' data mine". 1.2 also: "To spam, phish, pharm, pretext, spider, crawl, or scrape" and "To harass, abuse, insult, harm, defame, slander, disparage". Terms 2.3 invites notice of "any defamatory, false, or otherwise harmful information".
- Verdict: commercial use FORBIDDEN, redistribution forbidden, derivatives forbidden outside non-commercial editorial or academic use. A site with ads, affiliate links or paid alerts should not use the data without a written licence from InfluenceMap. Facts (for example "InfluenceMap's Carbon Majors report says X") may be reported with citation, but do not host or republish the dataset. Whether quoting headline figures in a monetised page needs consent: NOT CONFIRMED, ask info@carbonmajors.org.
- Limitations (methodology): production-based, not operational; state-owned double counting risk: "State-owned oil and gas companies typically report total production rather than their equity share. This practice can lead to a potential issue of double counting ... collating data from third-party sources is utilized to adjust". Mergers: "the acquired companies' emissions are attributed to the surviving company". Entity selection threshold: 8 million tonnes CO2 production (FAQ).

### 2.3 EU ETS (Union Registry, EUTL) and EEA data viewer

- URLs: https://climate.ec.europa.eu/areas-action/carbon-markets/eu-emissions-trading-system-eu-ets/union-registry_en ; Union Registry public site https://union-registry-data.ec.europa.eu/ (single-page app; file list read from its endpoint https://union-registry-data.ec.europa.eu/api/data-download) ; https://www.eea.europa.eu/en/analysis/maps-and-charts/emissions-trading-viewer-1-dashboards ; EEA catalogue record https://sdi.eea.europa.eu/catalogue/srv/api/records/385c4dc1-2c58-430a-9b9a-affc5b358101 ; https://commission.europa.eu/legal-notice_en .
- What it is: "The following information is publicly available on the Union Registry website: Data on free allocation, verified emissions, and compliance status of operators; Transactions taking place in the Registry, as well as data on account holders". Records "Annual verified CO2 emissions from operators". Covers stationary installations, aircraft operators (since 2012), maritime operators (since January 2024). EEA: "more than 16000 stationary installations, 1600 aircraft operators and 2600 maritime operators".
- Unit: installation or operator, with an account holder (company). Parent company field: NOT CONFIRMED (I did not open the data files, and no page I read lists a parent company column). Account holder names are not necessarily the ultimate parent.
- Scope: direct (scope 1) emissions of installations covered by the ETS. Reported by operators and described as "verified"; who verifies is not stated on the pages I read: NOT CONFIRMED.
- Years and cadence: Union Registry reports list "09/04/2026 - Verified Emissions for 2025" and "05/10/2026 - Compliance data for 2025", and annual verified emissions each April since 2013. Daily extracts exist: the file listing shows "Accounts Daily", "Operators Daily", "Operators Yearly Activity Daily", "Registry Holdings Daily" as .csv.gz on a public blob store, and a header check of Operators Daily showed Last-Modified Mon, 05 Oct 2026 and size 1,837,005 bytes. Verified emissions for 2025, 2024, 2023, 2022, 2021 are separate XLSX files; compliance 2021 to 2024 as XLSX. EEA viewer (extracted 1 July 2026): temporal coverage 2005 to 2024; aggregated by country, activity and year; the viewer is aggregate, not company level, and EEA adds scope estimates 2005 to 2020.
- Access: files by direct URL, no key, no documented rate limit (NOT CONFIRMED any limit). I did not download the data files, so column names are NOT CONFIRMED from a primary page.
- Licence: Commission legal notice: "Unless otherwise indicated ... content owned by the EU on this website is licensed under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International (CC BY 4.0) licence. This means that reuse is allowed, provided appropriate credit is given and changes are indicated." EEA catalogue record for the ETS dataset: "License CC-BY 4.0 ... Copyright holder: European Commission, European Environment Agency (EEA)." EEA viewer page: "re-use of content on the EEA website for commercial or non-commercial purposes is permitted free of charge, provided that the source is acknowledged". The Union Registry public site itself has no licence text that I could read (SPA): NOT CONFIRMED beyond the Commission-wide notice.
- Limitations: ETS thresholds and scope changed over time (EEA harmonises); installation-level not group-level; shipping and aviation reporting basis differs. Do not label a company as "an emitter of X" from an installation account holder without checking corporate group.

### 2.4 EU Industrial Emissions Portal (E-PRTR and EU Registry on Industrial Sites)

- URLs: https://industry.eea.europa.eu/ ; https://industry.eea.europa.eu/industrial-emissions/about ; https://industry.eea.europa.eu/industrial-emissions/dataset ; https://www.eea.europa.eu/en/datahub/datahubitem-view/9405f714-8015-4b5b-a63c-280b82861b3d ; metadata PDF (version 13) https://sdi.eea.europa.eu/catalogue/srv/api/records/ff47e25d-5d4c-491d-b9ce-de17ca61fe6d/attachments/EEA_Industrial_Reporting_Metadata_v13.pdf ; catalogue record JSON.
- What: "covers over 60 000 industrial sites from 65 economic activities across Europe". Includes greenhouse gases among 91 pollutants. Facilities report to national authorities; "The information contained in the portal is reported annually". Unit: facility, installation, large combustion plant; with parent company details in the EU Registry: the metadata lists parentCompanyName, parentCompanyURL (optional), and parentCompany_confidentialityReasonCode / Name ("Mandatory if confidentiality claimed"). So parent name is reported by the operator and can be withheld as confidential.
- Reported or estimated: reported by operators; there is a methodCode field "that classifies the quantity was estimated, calculated or measured" (metadata).
- Years: dataset "Ver 16.0 Feb. 2026", published 20 Feb 2026, temporal coverage 2007 to 2024 (datahub page last modified 25 Sept 2026). Annual cadence. About page gaps: "Norway did not report data under the EU Registry (2017 onwards)... Data for Liechtenstein (2018 - 2023); Czechia, Lithuania, Malta, Slovakia, Montenegro and Switzerland (2023 - 2024); Cyprus (2024) is incomplete." Geography: EU Member States, Iceland, Liechtenstein, Montenegro, Norway, Serbia, Switzerland and Northern Ireland.
- Reporting thresholds: facility must exceed activity capacity thresholds and pollutant release thresholds (Annex II) to report a pollutant, so small emitters are absent. The E-PRTR Regulation is superseded by Regulation 2024/1244 with reporting at installation level from 2028 ("The requirements of the new legislation will apply from 2028").
- Access: direct download (CSV, TXT, SQL, XLS/XLSX; spatial GeoPackage, GDB) from the datahub; ESRI REST and OGC WMS services; no key needed. API for JSON: NOT CONFIRMED.
- Licence: EEA catalogue record: "License CC-BY 4.0 (https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). Copyright holder: European Environment Agency (EEA)." Commercial use and derivatives allowed with attribution.
- Limitations: parent names are free text as reported and can be absent or confidential; facility-level only; a "parent" here is a reported string, not a verified corporate tree.

### 2.5 US EPA Greenhouse Gas Reporting Program (GHGRP, FLIGHT, Envirofacts)

- URLs read: https://www.epa.gov/ghgreporting ; https://www.epa.gov/ghgreporting/data-sets ; https://www.epa.gov/ghgreporting/ghgrp-reported-data ; https://www.epa.gov/enviro/greenhouse-gas-overview ; https://www.epa.gov/enviro/envirofacts-data-service-api ; https://www.epa.gov/web-policies-and-procedures/epa-disclaimers ; FLIGHT https://ghgdata.epa.gov/flight/ (interactive); live API calls to https://data.epa.gov/efservice/ .
- Parent companies: yes. Data Sets page: "Reported Parent Companies (xlsb): Each facility subject to the GHGRP is required to report their highest level U.S. Parent Company(s) as part of their annual report. This file lists each facility's reported parent company(s) and percent ownership." Note "U.S." parent only, so foreign parents are not captured by this wording. The Envirofacts table PUB_DIM_FACILITY has a `parent_company` text field, for example "PUGET HOLDINGS LLC (100%)" for facility 1000001 in 2023 and a multi-owner string for 2010 ("Empeco IV, LLC and USPF II Ferndale Holdings, LLC (74.33298%); Diamond Generating Corporation (14.00002%); Tenaska Energy, Inc. ... (11.667%)"). Parent names vary by year (string field), so group by entity carefully.
- Measured: reported, facility level (direct emitters), plus suppliers' potential emissions: "Suppliers report the quantity of GHGs that would be emitted if the fuels and industrial gases that they place into the economy each year are used/released." Do not add supplier numbers to facility emissions (overlap; my inference from this definition).
- Coverage and threshold: "facilities that directly emit 25,000 metric tons of carbon dioxide equivalent or more per year are required to submit annual reports". RY2023: "7,544 facilities in nine industry sectors reported direct emissions; Reported direct emissions totaled 2.58 billion metric tons CO2e". "The GHGRP data collected from direct emitters represent about half of all U.S. emissions. When including greenhouse gas information reported ... by suppliers, emissions coverage reaches approximately 85-90%". Biogenic CO2 excluded; GWPs "generally based on the IPCC's Fourth Assessment Report" for the RY2023 summary.
- Verification: "All reports submitted to EPA are evaluated by electronic validation and verification checks." Reporters choose among prescribed methods; so this is self-reported, EPA-checked, not third-party audited (the page does not mention third-party audit).
- Latest year and release schedule: EPA page says "the reported data are made available to the public in October of each year", but the main page still highlights "2023 Data Highlights" and Data Sets page (last updated July 30, 2026) lists "2023 Data Summary Spreadsheets". ## 3. Cautions when naming companies

Most of these are drawn from the source pages above. Where something is my own editorial suggestion it is labelled so.

1. Say what the number is. Each source differs and a reader (or a lawyer) will check:
   - Reported by the company to a regulator: EPA GHGRP (self-reported, EPA checks), Canada GHGRP, Australia NGER, Japan system, E-PRTR. EU ETS is described as "verified emissions".
   - Estimated by a third party: Climate TRACE ("modeled"), GEM coal plant CO2 (computed from capacity, emission factor, heat rate, country capacity factor), Carbon Mapper (remote sensing), Carbon Majors (from reported production and factors).
   - Phrase estimates as "Climate TRACE estimates that ...", "modelled", "estimated", and reported figures as "reported to the EU ETS", "reported to EPA under the GHGRP". Do not write "emitted" as an established fact for modelled values. (editorial suggestion, consistent with Climate TRACE's own "Exercise caution" advice and its confidence ratings)
2. Always show year, unit, gas basis and scope next to each number. Examples of differences: EPA RY2023 uses AR4 GWPs and excludes biogenic CO2; Canada uses AR5 and reports biomass CO2 separately; Climate TRACE offers 100-year and 20-year CO2e; Carbon Majors is mostly scope 3 category 11 plus partial scope 1 ("An entity's total scope 1 emissions may be higher"); NGER scope 2 is location-based with market-based optional; EPA supplier data are potential emissions. Never add scope 1 and scope 3 numbers into one ranking.
3. Ownership and parent attribution is where fairness problems start:
   - Climate TRACE: only 32% of global emissions have full ownership trees and 14% immediate owners only; CSV ownership uses shortest path only; API gives owner names but not shares; its own article says assigning responsibility (immediate owner, parent, investors, offtaker) is left to users. Observed: the /sources by-owner list shows full-asset emissions to every co-owner (same 27.6 Mt appeared for both Shell PLC and Equinor ASA filters), so summing by owner double counts.
   - GHG Protocol: scope 1 depends on equity share versus control consolidation; a joint venture can belong to several companies or none under a given method.
   - Carbon Majors: double counting between state-owned companies and multinationals is a documented risk.
   - EPA: "highest level U.S. Parent" and percentages are strings that change by year; E-PRTR parent names are free text and may be confidential; EU ETS account holders are not necessarily parents.
   - Name matching: Climate TRACE returns many separate legal entities for "Shell". Use LEI or entity ID, not text matching, and display "owned X percent by" with the stake and the source.
4. Coverage gaps imply unfairness: absence from a list does not mean low emissions. Thresholds (EPA 25,000 t; Canada 10 kt; Japan energy and emissions thresholds; E-PRTR capacity and release thresholds), missing countries (Norway not in EU Registry since 2017; several countries incomplete for 2023 and 2024 in E-PRTR), and excluded sectors (agriculture and forestry in NGER) mean many emitters are not shown. Say "among facilities that report" or "among assets modelled by Climate TRACE".
5. Staleness: EPA has not released RY2024; E-PRTR latest is 2024 (published Feb 2026); EU ETS verified emissions for 2025 were released 9 April 2026; Climate TRACE monthly data lag about two months. Show "data year" and "retrieved on" and re-pull data on the source's cadence (annual files each spring or autumn, Climate TRACE monthly). Reprints of old figures under a company name are a typical source of complaints.
6. Defamation and false-statement exposure (research findings, not legal advice):
   - UK Defamation Act 2013 (per legislation.gov.uk text returned by search; the page itself blocked scripted fetch): section 1: "harm to the reputation of a body that trades for profit is not 'serious harm' unless it has caused or is likely to cause the body serious financial loss"; section 2: truth is a defence if "the imputation conveyed by the statement complained of is substantially true". Takeaway: accuracy and the exact imputation matter. A table of emissions is low risk if it faithfully reproduces a stated source; risk rises with headlines such as "worst polluter", "responsible for", "violating", or implying illegality or concealment (editorial suggestion).
   - Other jurisdictions (US, India, EU states, where readers and the owner may be): NOT CONFIRMED. The site owner's country and the company's country affect which rules apply. A short consultation with a media lawyer before launch is advisable (editorial suggestion).
   - Emitting greenhouse gases within permits and under the ETS is lawful; do not present emissions numbers as wrongdoing.
7. Cite method and year in the interface: the sources ask for this. Climate TRACE requires attribution and "indicate if you have made any changes"; Canada requires the OGL attribution statement; Australia requires its attribution text; GEM and EEA require credit. Include dataset version (for example "Climate TRACE v5.11.0"), the retrieval date, and a link to the method page.
8. Right of reply and corrections. The IPSO Editors' Code (clause 1, as in the Editors' Codebook): "A fair opportunity to reply to significant inaccuracies should be given, when reasonably called for" and the press "must distinguish clearly between comment, conjecture and fact." It binds IPSO-regulated publishers, so use it as a best-practice benchmark, not a legal duty. Data providers themselves run correction channels: Climate TRACE ("Share feedback" on each source card; changes only on independently verifiable information), Urgewald ("we specifically invite companies to notify us of any errors"), InfluenceMap (Terms 2.3 asks to be notified of "any defamatory, false, or otherwise harmful information"). Recommended for the site (editorial suggestion): a visible "Report an error or request a correction" link on each company or facility page, a documented response time, a corrections log, and a way to attach a company statement.
9. Do not use company logos or imply endorsement: Canada's OGL excludes logos and implied endorsement; Climate TRACE's logo is a registered trademark; InfluenceMap forbids suggesting endorsement. Use plain text names.
10. Licence traps when money is involved. Forbidden for a monetised site without a written licence: Carbon Majors, CDP, SBTi (beyond non-commercial), TPI (revenue-related use), Urgewald GCEL, GHG Protocol text, Kayrros (subscriber terms). Carbon Mapper is non-commercial (the status of ad revenue is NOT CONFIRMED). Pollution Inventory has a one-year, internal-use style conditional licence. EPA data terms are unclear for commercial use. Safe by explicit text: Climate TRACE (CC BY 4.0), Canada (OGL-Canada), EU ETS and E-PRTR (CC BY 4.0 per EC and EEA), CER Australia (CC BY 4.0), GEM (CC BY 4.0), Japan (PDL 1.0 compatible with CC BY 4.0).
11. Embedded third-party rows: Climate TRACE says some rows reproduce data from EDGAR, FAOSTAT, E-PRTR, EPA FLIGHT, LMOP, Israel PRTR and the Canada GHGRP "which may have different specific terms". For a clean licence chain, prefer pulling facility rows for those sectors directly from the original open source.
12. Wording examples consistent with the sources (editorial):
   - Reported: "In 2023, EPA's Greenhouse Gas Reporting Program lists the Baytown plant (reported parent: [name], [share]) at [X] million tonnes CO2e of direct emissions (reported by the facility; excludes biogenic CO2)."
   - Estimated: "Climate TRACE, which models emissions from satellite and other data, estimates that [asset], listed as owned by [owner] ([share] where available), emitted about [X] Mt CO2e in 2024 (confidence: [medium]). Ownership data are incomplete."
   - Always add: "This is not a statement that the company breached any law."

## 4. Best six sources for an automated, commercially usable "who emits what" page

1. Climate TRACE (emissions + ownership). Why: global asset coverage, owners and parents for about a third of global emissions (plus immediate owners for 14%), a keyless JSON API with owner search and owner filter, monthly updates, CC BY 4.0 that explicitly allows commercial use, derivatives and redistribution, and per-gas confidence ratings you can display. Caveats: modelled, beta API, ownership partial, API has no ownership percentage, owner filter shows whole-asset emissions, embedded third-party rows.
2. US EPA GHGRP via Envirofacts. Why: reported facility emissions with a parent_company string and ownership percentages, free JSON API, large and important geography. Caveats: RY2023 is the latest published, program may be cut back, commercial reuse wording needs a legal read, parent field is US-parent text.
3. EU ETS Union Registry (with EEA CC BY 4.0 record). Why: installation-level verified emissions, daily operator extract, annual verified emissions each April, clear EU reuse licence. Caveats: no confirmed parent field, columns not verified by me, XLSX and CSV files rather than an API.
4. Canada GHGRP (Open Government Licence - Canada). Why: the clearest explicit commercial-use grant, RY2024 available, facility-level, company name searchable, CKAN API. Caveats: parent company not confirmed, 10 kt threshold, biomass CO2 separate.
5. Australia NGER (Clean Energy Regulator, CC BY 4.0). Why: corporation-level scope 1 and scope 2, facility-level electricity and Safeguard data, FY2024-25 published Feb 2026, CSV files. Caveats: control basis, republications, page-clone clause.
6. EU Industrial Emissions Portal (E-PRTR, CC-BY 4.0). Why: facility-level reported emissions through 2024 with parentCompanyName and method code, wide European coverage, open downloads. Caveats: thresholds, parent field optional or confidential, incomplete countries for recent years.

Honourable mentions: Global Energy Monitor (CC BY 4.0, best owner and parent data for fossil and heavy industry, modelled emissions, form-gated download) as a supplement to Climate TRACE ownership; Japan's system (PDL 1.0, FY2023, company and site level, Japanese only, no API confirmed).
