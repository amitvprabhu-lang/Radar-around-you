How these numbers are made

  • Each number is worked out by the same code as its page, from the same feed, and the time beside it says what it is: the time given in the feed, the time of a Kp period or of the newest advisory or detection, or for asteroid close approaches (and storms when none is active) the day our collector read the list. For GDACS events it is the time of GDACS's newest update. For rocket launches it is when our collector read the list. For tonight's sky, the time is MET Norway's forecast time for the city. For the ISS, the time is the satellite data time. For the catalogue counts, the time is when CelesTrak last updated the catalogue file. It is never the time this page was built.
  • Each page has a limit on how old its data may be: earthquakes 3 hours, Kp 8 hours, storms 12 hours, fire detections 8 hours, asteroid close approaches 48 hours, satellite data for the Starlink page 30 hours, GDACS events 6 hours, rocket launches 6 hours, the cloud forecast of the sky pages 6 hours, the ISS page 30 hours and the catalogue 72 hours. A page whose data is older is not updated and is not linked from here.
  • Nothing here is a forecast of ours or a warning. Each page names its source agency and links to it.

Questions

How fresh are these numbers?

Each has its own data time in the table. The newest is 11 October 2026, 03:59 UTC and the oldest 10 October 2026, 08:25 UTC.

Why does a page show no number?

Its data was older than the page's limit, or was not available when this page was built, so the page was not updated. Its last copy may still be on the site, with its own data time.

Sources

  • USGS earthquake GeoJSON summary feeds. The magnitude 2.5 and above, past 7 days feed this page reads
  • NOAA SWPC data access. Where the Kp and solar wind files are listed
  • NASA JPL Close-Approach Data API. The list of close approaches and its default query

  • NASA FIRMS. The 24 hour global VIIRS fire files
  • GDACS, the Global Disaster Alert and Coordination System. A cooperation framework between the United Nations and the European Commission
  • The Space Devs: Launch Library 2. The launch list this page reads, by The Space Devs
  • MET Norway Locationforecast. The hourly cloud forecast (total cloud cover for all heights). Data from The Norwegian Meteorological Institute, shortened MET Norway
  • CelesTrak current GP data. The ISS element set (NORAD 25544)
  • CelesTrak SATCAT. The whole satellite catalogue, read once a day as one CSV file (celestrak.org/pub/satcat.csv)

Radar Around You is a free tool for looking up: what is overhead, what is in tonight's sky and what the ground has just done. Satellite and quake data come from CelesTrak and the USGS. Positions of the Moon, Sun and planets are computed with the astronomy-engine library. Every number on these pages links to how it was found on How we know.

Source code and data notes on GitHub. Code under the MIT licence; data keeps its sources' terms.
