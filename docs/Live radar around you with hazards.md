# Lead with the sky, relay the hazards

Build the "live radar around you" as one 3D globe that opens on the visitor's own spot, but launch it with the layers that are legally clean, global, cheap to run and always have something to show: **the ISS and bright satellites, planes overhead, your aurora chance tonight, the Moon, planets and meteor showers, and recent earthquakes**, plus a world ticker of storms, fires and eruptions from GDACS and NASA EONET. Add wildfires, cyclone tracks, volcanic ash and lightning next (clean lightning data only exists for the Americas and Europe), and leave official warnings, tsunami messages, air quality, heat, floods and landslides for a later phase in which you relay the issuer's words unchanged. The combined idea has almost no search demand of its own: in your Ubersuggest data "what is above me right now" gets **90 US searches a month** and "live earth map" 2,400. LiveEarthViewer's "My Sky, My Ground" page already offers a rough text-panel version. So your edge has to come from execution: a fast, mobile-first globe, one worldwide alert stream that fires rarely, and per-layer live tool pages that catch real demand such as "aurora forecast" (**201,000, with 80 percent of searches clicking a result**), "northern lights tonight" and "meteor shower tonight" (165,000 each) and "lightning map" (74,000). Earthquakes look bigger (450,000 for "earthquake today"), but more than half of those searches end without a click, with Google's own quake panel at the top, and earthquaketrack.com, which holds three of the top ten results, lost roughly three quarters of its estimated search traffic in a year. Money splits the same way: Google's sensitive-event rules and advertisers' brand-safety filters make disaster spikes good for reach but poor for ad revenue, so the calm, beautiful sky layers are your revenue base and the hazard layers are your trust and reach base. Technically, keep WordPress for pages, serve the globe as a static app on the same domain behind Cloudflare's free plan, and fetch live data with a real server cron or a Cloudflare scheduled Worker that writes cached JSON, never with WP-Cron. You can start without buying anything if your host allows real cron jobs; budget later for Cloudflare Workers Paid ($5 a month), a paid or self-hosted weather API before ads go on, and a small server when heavy map layers arrive. **Nothing here guarantees success.** All keyword and traffic figures are third-party estimates, and this is research, not legal advice.

## Launch with the sky and quakes; hazards follow in waves

Every layer was judged on five tests. Is the licence clean for a site that will later carry ads? Does it cover the whole world? Can one scheduled job fetch it cheaply? Does it look spectacular in 3D? And is it information rather than a warning? The sky layers and earthquakes pass all five. Their sources are US public domain or Creative Commons Attribution, they refresh within minutes, and planes and satellites guarantee there is always something moving above every visitor, which hazard layers cannot promise. Most hazard layers fail at least one test: patchy coverage, heavy processing, zone geometry that has to be joined by hand, or the legal weight of looking like an official warning. The verdict scale below is **clean** (commercial use allowed), **grey area** (non-commercial clauses or terms that could not be found) and **restricted** (terms that forbid ads, redistribution or public display without a deal). "Relay" means your own server has to fetch and cache the feed, either because the browser cannot read it directly (no CORS header) or because the provider asks you not to let browsers call it.

### Above and tonight: clean data with strict fetching rules

Satellites come from CelesTrak, but its rules are strict. It updates orbital data only every two hours, asks you to download "only once per update", and firewalls an IP after 50 error responses in two hours ([CelesTrak usage policy](https://celestrak.org/usage-policy.php); [CelesTrak GP formats](https://celestrak.org/NORAD/documentation/gp-data-formats.php)). Redistribution rests on Space-Track's **blanket approval for orbital elements, the satellite catalogue and re-entry data, conditioned on citation** ([Space-Track documentation](https://www.space-track.org/documentation)). One recent change matters: the five-digit catalogue numbers ran out on **11 July 2026**, so new objects exist only in the newer OMM format, and code that reads only the old TLE format silently drops every recent Starlink batch ([CelesTrak](https://celestrak.org/)). Planes come from adsb.lol, whose live API and history are ODbL. That allows commercial use, but the owner asks production users to get in touch and plans an API key for feeders ([adsb.lol OpenAPI](https://api.adsb.lol/api/openapi.json)). The alternatives are worse: OpenSky now needs a written agreement for any live product, and airplanes.live limits its API to feeders or approved projects ([OpenSky terms](https://opensky-network.org/about/terms-of-use); [airplanes.live API guide](https://airplanes.live/api-guide/)). Coverage is the honest weakness. A 2023 analysis found Asian clusters "nowhere near representative" and almost no positions in the Gulf outside Dubai ([Mark Litwintschik](https://tech.marksblogg.com/global-flight-tracking-adsb.html)), so show a coverage meter rather than pretend the count is a census. adsb.lol's API answers by point and radius, so every visitor's spot is a separate query; cache by coarse grid cell and rate-limit, or a viral day becomes a flood of upstream calls.

Aurora is the easiest layer of all. NOAA's OVATION grid is public domain, readable straight from the browser, cached for 60 seconds and refreshed about every five minutes, with a lead time of roughly 30 to 90 minutes ([OVATION JSON](https://services.swpc.noaa.gov/json/ovation_aurora_latest.json); [SWPC aurora forecast](https://www.swpc.noaa.gov/products/aurora-30-minute-forecast)). NOAA's older solar-wind JSON paths now return 404, so build on the newer real-time feeds ([retired SWPC path](https://services.swpc.noaa.gov/products/solar-wind/plasma-7-day.json)). "Can I see it?" also needs cloud cover. Open-Meteo is the easy option, but its free API is for non-commercial use only and names "websites or apps that have subscriptions or display advertisements" as commercial ([Open-Meteo terms](https://open-meteo.com/en/terms)). You can use the free tier while the site has no ads, but you must move to a paid plan, self-host it, or process ECMWF's open data (CC BY 4.0) yourself before ads go on ([Open-Meteo pricing](https://open-meteo.com/en/pricing); [ECMWF open data](https://www.ecmwf.int/en/forecasts/datasets/open-data)). Meteor data is fragmented: the Global Meteor Network is CC BY 4.0 and updates every six hours, JPL's fireball API is weeks behind and forbids embedding in websites, and the American Meteor Society is still recovering from a cyberattack ([GMN data](https://globalmeteornetwork.org/data/); [JPL SSD API](https://ssd-api.jpl.nasa.gov/); [AMS notice](https://www.amsmeteors.org/ams-programs/fireball-api/)).

### Under and around: the clean global backbone, and its holes

Earthquakes are the cleanest hazard. USGS feeds are public domain and update every minute, and EMSC pushes new events over a websocket under CC BY 4.0 ([USGS feeds](https://earthquake.usgs.gov/earthquakes/feed/v1.0/geojson.php); [EMSC real-time](https://www.seismicportal.eu/realtime.html)). The USGS feed for significant quakes already links ShakeMap intensity contours and PAGER impact levels, all open to the browser ([USGS significant feed](https://earthquake.usgs.gov/earthquakes/feed/v1.0/summary/significant_month.geojson)). Earthquake early warning is different: ShakeAlert messages go only to licensed partners ([ShakeAlert licensing](https://www.shakealert.org/technical-partner-resources/shakealert-licensing-center/)).

For everything else, two feeds form a free global backbone. GDACS, run by the UN and the European Commission, offers a JSON API that the browser can read, covering earthquakes, cyclones, floods, volcanoes and wildfires, with cyclone tracks and wind polygons that already merge NHC and JTWC data; on 4 October 2026 it listed **17 cyclone events** ([GDACS API](https://www.gdacs.org/gdacsapi/api/events/geteventlist/SEARCH?eventlist=TC)). Its licence is not stated on its own pages, but the humanitarian data portal lists it as CC BY and the Commission's legal notice covers EU content under CC BY 4.0 ([HDX GDACS](https://data.humdata.org/dataset/gdacs-rss-information); [European Commission legal notice](https://commission.europa.eu/legal-notice_en)). NASA EONET listed **about 7,200 open events** that day, almost all wildfires ([EONET open events](https://eonet.gsfc.nasa.gov/api/v3/events?status=open)). Both say plainly that they are not official, which suits a consumer tool ([GDACS terms](https://www.gdacs.org/About/termofuse.aspx); [What is EONET](https://eonet.gsfc.nasa.gov/what-is-eonet)).

The holes are specific. **Lightning has no clean worldwide feed.** NOAA's GOES lightning mapper covers the Americas up to 52 degrees north and EUMETSAT's MTG imager covers Europe and Africa, while the community network Blitzortung forbids commercial projects and says outright "Do not setup yet another visualization of online data" ([GOES-R GLM](https://www.goes-r.gov/spacesegment/glm.html); [EUMETSAT MTG access](https://user.eumetsat.int/resources/user-guides/mtg-data-access-guide); [Blitzortung terms](https://www.blitzortung.org/en/contact.php)). **There is no clean global rain radar either.** RainViewer cut its free API back between 2025 and January 2026 to "personal and educational use only", with a maximum zoom of 7 and 100 requests per IP per minute, and Europe's OPERA composite needs a licence ([RainViewer API](https://www.rainviewer.com/api.html); [RainViewer transition](https://www.rainviewer.com/api/transition-faq.html); [EUMETNET OPERA](https://www.eumetnet.eu/activities/observations-programme/current-activities/opera/)). **Official alerts are mostly zones, not shapes.** Of 341 active US alerts on 4 October 2026, only 50 carried a polygon, and MeteoAlarm's keyless feeds use region codes without polygons ([NWS active alerts](https://api.weather.gov/alerts/active); [MeteoAlarm feeds](https://feeds.meteoalarm.org/)). Cyclone sources vary by basin: NHC is public domain but sends no CORS header, JTWC's feed is open with a notice requirement, Australia's Bureau of Meteorology bans commercial use, and India's IMD requires registration with no licence found ([NHC disclaimer](https://www.nhc.noaa.gov/disclaimer.shtml); [JTWC RSS](https://www.metoc.navy.mil/jtwc/rss/jtwc.rss); [BoM copyright](https://www.bom.gov.au/other/copyright.shtml); [IMD API](https://api.imd.gov.in/)). Fires should be built on VIIRS now, because NASA plans to stop collecting Terra MODIS data in January 2027 and Aqua MODIS around September 2027 ([CAMS on MODIS](https://atmosphere.copernicus.eu/drifting-away-modis-cams-global-fire-assimilation-system)). Landslide nowcasts are clean but arrive as daily files of about 145 to 195 MiB, which only a server can process ([LHASA files](https://portal.nccs.nasa.gov/datashare/landslides/nrt/hazard/)).

### Sources to keep off an ad-supported site

Several popular sources block an ad-supported site no matter how "non-commercial" is read: Open-Meteo's free tier, RainViewer, Blitzortung, the WAQI air-quality network ("Public usage by for-profit corporations requires explicit agreement"), ReliefWeb ("personal, non-commercial use" with no right to redistribute), PDC's DisasterAWARE (no redistribution under its July 2026 terms) and Australia's BoM ([WAQI API](https://aqicn.org/api/); [ReliefWeb terms](https://reliefweb.int/terms-conditions); [DisasterAWARE terms](https://www.disasteraware.com/terms-conditions)). MIROVA's volcano hotspots and the Smithsonian volcano site sit in the grey zone, inviting only non-commercial use ([MIROVA credits](https://www.mirovaweb.it/NRT/credits.php); [GVP citation and terms](https://volcano.si.edu/gvp_cite.cfm)). This matters competitively: LiveEarthViewer's personal page takes its weather and air quality from Open-Meteo, so it would need a paid plan or another source if it ever ran ads; whether it already pays is unknown ([LEV My Sky, My Ground](https://liveearthviewer.com/here)).

About half of the clean feeds (GDACS, EONET, USGS, HANS, WFIGS, the European fire maps, JMA, NOAA SWPC, NASA GIBS) can be read straight from the browser, and the rest need your own server. Route all of them through your cache anyway. Rate limits are unpublished for most of them, and US federal data programmes went through decommissions and budget fights from 2025 into 2026, so a server that watches each feed's freshness and hides stale layers protects users from a map that silently shows yesterday's world ([NESDIS 2025 notice of changes](https://www.nesdis.noaa.gov/about/documents-reports/notice-of-changes/2025-notice-of-changes)). European sources on CC BY terms (GDACS, the Copernicus fire, air and mapping services) are a useful hedge.

## LiveEarthViewer got there first, so execution is the moat

The honest starting point is that the core idea already exists in rough form. LiveEarthViewer's "My Sky, My Ground" page (liveearthviewer.com/here) runs only when the visitor asks and lays out **nine panels**: sky and space with the ISS and satellites, NOAA aurora probability, aircraft from ADS-B watched "in a fixed set of circles, not the whole planet", USGS earthquakes, US tide gauges, weather and air quality via Open-Meteo, the ocean, the Sun, Moon and Mars, and geographic curiosities. It states that coordinates are worked out in the browser and never sent to its server ([LEV My Sky, My Ground](https://liveearthviewer.com/here)). Its home page is a live globe that fuses almost every layer in your plan, from radar, lightning and storm tracks to wildfires, tsunamis, floods, flights and Starlink ([LiveEarthViewer](https://liveearthviewer.com/)). It also runs a "use my location" sky page that fuses aurora with cloud, Moon and darkness, plus SEO city pages such as "What's in the Sky Tonight: Los Angeles" ([LEV Sky](https://liveearthviewer.com/sky); [LEV Sky Tonight LA](https://liveearthviewer.com/sky/tonight/los-angeles)). What it lacks is just as clear: the personal view is a page of text panels rather than the main globe experience, it leaves out wildfires, lightning, storms, floods, volcanoes and official alerts, it has no push alerts and no native app, and no traffic figures could be found, so its traction is unknown.

The incumbents with real reach each own one band. **MyRadar** is the closest by breadth: radar centred on you, earthquakes, a worldwide hurricane layer, US wildfires, NWS alerts and an aviation layer that tracks flights. It reports about 13 million monthly users, a self-reported figure, and runs ads plus a premium tier ([MyRadar on Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.acmeaom.android.myradar); [SpaceNews](https://spacenews.com/acme-myradar-constellation/)). Wikipedia says it added ISS tracking in early 2020, although its current store listing does not mention it ([Wikipedia: MyRadar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MyRadar)). **Watch Duty** is the breakout hazard app: **16.8 million yearly active users in 2025**, 1.17 billion pageviews, no ads, funded mostly by **135,223 paying members**, with US floods added in June 2026 and international expansion being prepared for 2027 ([Watch Duty 2025 annual report](https://www.watchduty.org/blog/2025-annual-report); [Watch Duty floods](https://www.watchduty.org/blog/watch-duty-launches-flooding-nationwide)). **Windy** (50M+ Android installs, free and ad-free with a Premium tier) has lightning, fires, air quality and official alerts, while **Zoom Earth** (5M+, ads plus Pro) and **Ventusky** (5M+, with an aurora probability layer and lightning alerts within a chosen distance) cover storms and fires ([Windy on Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.windyty.android); [Zoom Earth on Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.neave.zoomearth); [Ventusky on Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=cz.ackee.ventusky)). None of them shows satellites, meteor showers or a "what is overhead now" view.

The sky side is served piece by piece. **Orbital Radar** covers satellites, debris, space weather and aurora, with pass alerts and a daily email, free and funded by donations ([Orbital Radar](https://orbitalradar.com/)). Your Ubersuggest export shows it growing from near zero before May 2026 to about **209,600 estimated US search visits a month** in September, with domain authority 39 and about 8,100 backlinks (Ubersuggest export supplied by you, 4 Oct 2026). **SkyAR** is a browser AR app showing planes, satellites, stars and an aurora Kp reading, free with donations and built with AI-assisted coding; **AboveMe** shows satellites, aircraft and the night sky over your spot with no cookies ([SkyAR](https://skyar.app/); [AboveMe](https://aboveme.org/)). Neither touches ground hazards. Then come the 2026 web globes: overwatch.earth (launched 6 June 2026 with 31 to 35 layers and Proton VPN affiliate placements), Metiq (a Show HN that scored 148 points, where commenters noted that "90% of all initial traffic is mobile" on a site not built for phones), PocketWorld and The World Now, all planet-centred rather than personal ([Tuxxin launch post](https://tuxxin.com/blog/introducing-overwatch-earth-live-planet-globe); [Hacker News: Metiq](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48556082); [PocketWorld](https://pocketworld.org/live-earth); [The World Now](https://www.the-world-now.com/global-disaster-tracker)). Breadth of layers is now cheap and no longer a differentiator.

The real gap is narrower than "nobody has built this", but it is real. First, **nobody with reach makes the personal frame the main experience**: one view that starts at your location and answers what is above you, what is in your sky tonight, what is under you and what is around you, at a glance. Second, **alerts are split by hazard or by country**. One developer, JRustonApps, runs three separate apps for aurora (5M+ installs), earthquakes (10M+) and lightning (1M+), which shows demand exists for each layer and that nobody has bundled them ([My Aurora Forecast](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.jrustonapps.myauroraforecast); [My Earthquake Alerts](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.jrustonapps.myearthquakealerts); [My Lightning Tracker](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.jrustonapps.mylightningtracker)). Watch Duty, Japan's NERV and the FEMA app are each limited to one country. Third, **no product shows sky and hazards in one visual**, zooming from orbit through the air to the ground and below it around one pin. Fourth, the near-competitors are desktop-leaning web pages. The defensibility warning is serious: the two most dangerous moves would be LiveEarthViewer turning its /here page into a polished, globe-first mobile experience with alerts, or MyRadar adding satellites and aurora to a map that 13 million people already open. Your advantages have to be speed, design, honesty about coverage, a one-glance summary, rare but useful alerts, and shareability. Ads are normal in this category (MyRadar, Zoom Earth, Flightradar24, My Aurora Forecast and Earthquake Network all run them), but ad-free trust is a selling point for safety alerts, as Watch Duty, LastQuake and NERV show.

## Open on "near you", count what is there, alert rarely

### The first ten seconds decide whether anyone stays

Never ask for location on arrival. Chrome's data shows that **77 percent of desktop permission prompts appear with no sign of user intent, and only 12 percent of those are allowed, against 30 percent after an interaction** ([web.dev permissions best practices](https://web.dev/articles/permissions-best-practices)). Open instead on an approximate "near [city]" view derived from the visitor's IP at the edge; Cloudflare's Workers expose city, latitude and longitude on all plans ([Cloudflare request docs](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/runtime-apis/request/)). Offer a "Not here? Search" box and a clear "Use my exact location" button. Chrome 144 (January 2026) shipped an in-page `<geolocation>` element that defaults to approximate accuracy; in Chrome's trials, one site saw a 20 percent rise in successful location grants and another saw 54.4 percent of previously denied users grant permission again ([Chrome: rethinking web permissions](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/rethinking-web-permissions)). Google's own proposal defines approximate as coarser than 2 km, which is plenty for planes, satellites, aurora and quakes within 100 km ([approximate geolocation explainer](https://github.com/explainers-by-googlers/approximate-geolocation)).

Speed is the other half. Google's 2016 data found 53 percent of mobile visits abandoned after three seconds, and faster sites earned up to double the ad revenue; the data is old and from 3G networks, so treat it as directional ([Google: the need for mobile speed](https://blog.google/products/admanager/the-need-for-mobile-speed/)). The current target is a Largest Contentful Paint within 2.5 seconds ([web.dev Web Vitals](https://web.dev/articles/vitals)). So render a text answer before the globe finishes loading: "Near Leeds now: 14 planes overhead, ISS passes at 19:42, aurora chance 3 percent, no quakes within 300 km this week." That sentence is also your accessible alternative and your share-card text. Load ad scripts only after the core view.

### Four bands, live counters, and cards that name their source

Organise the radar into four bands that match the question in the visitor's head: **Above** (planes, satellites, the ISS), **Tonight** (aurora, Moon, planets, meteor showers, cloud), **Under** (earthquakes, volcanoes) and **Around** (storms, fires, lightning, alerts). The 3D design should earn the "exciting to look at" goal in one camera move: start in orbit with satellites on their real paths, fall through the air layer where planes glide at their true altitudes, and stop at the ground, where quakes pulse as rings with a cut-away showing their depth below the surface. Satellites should glow only when sunlit over a dark observer, which is when people can actually see them. NASA's yearly night-lights composites are available under CC0 for the night side of the globe ([lightpollutionmap.info help](https://www.lightpollutionmap.info/help.html)). Respect the phone setting for reduced motion, provide a keyboard-friendly list view of everything near the user, and pair hazard colours with icons and text, as guidance on accessible web maps requires ([Minnesota IT accessibility guide](https://mn.gov/mnit/assets/Accessibility%20Guide%20for%20Interactive%20Web%20Maps_tcm38-403564.pdf)).

Live counters should describe the world, not the audience: "214 planes and 31 satellites above you right now" is honest, personal and screenshot-friendly. The research found no credible evidence that "47 people are viewing this" counters raise engagement; the claims come from vendors selling them, and fake counts are a trust risk. Every item card should carry the source first, then the event time, "updated X minutes ago", distance from you, automatic or reviewed status where the source provides it, and a link to the official page. In Swiss workshops on hazard apps, participants rated **scientific experts 4.79 and state institutions 4.71 out of 5 for trust, against 2.71 for private institutions**, and wanted to know the source of every message ([Dallo and Marti 2021](https://www.research-collection.ethz.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/83e50c80-409c-4248-94dd-e1dd4a0b6b7f/content)). A private tool borrows trust by naming its sources visibly. Express chances the way NOAA does, with conditions attached ("aurora chance 15 percent if skies are dark and clear"), because NOAA itself warns that its lead time disappears when solar wind data is missing ([SWPC aurora forecast](https://www.swpc.noaa.gov/products/aurora-30-minute-forecast)). When a feed goes stale, grey it out and say so.

### Alerts as rare subscriptions, not a feed

The strongest retention loop in this category is "set your place once, get a rare but relevant alert". Watch Duty brands its rule "Zero Noise" and alerts only "if we see a potential threat to life or property" ([Watch Duty: how it works](https://www.watchduty.org/how-it-works/overview)). The web platform now punishes noise. Chrome says **fewer than 1 percent of web notifications get any interaction**, and since October 2025 it automatically revokes notification permission from sites with high volume and low engagement, while exempting installed web apps ([Google: automatic notification permission](https://blog.google/chromium/automatic-notification-permission/)). Since January 2026 it also rate-limits sites flagged as disruptive, with penalties of 1, 7 and then 14 days ([Chrome web push rate limits](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/web-push-rate-limits)). The Reuters Institute found that 79 percent of people get no news alerts in a typical week and 43 percent of those had switched them off ([CNN on the Reuters study](https://www.cnn.com/2025/06/20/media/news-alerts-disable-reuters-study-intl)). Hazard-app users want to pick their own regions, hazards and severity levels, and want it obvious whether a message is a warning or information about an ongoing or past event ([Dallo and Marti 2021](https://www.research-collection.ethz.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/83e50c80-409c-4248-94dd-e1dd4a0b6b7f/content)). The design follows directly: every alert is a threshold the user switches on from a specific card, off by default, with a frequency cap and one of three labels.

On iPhone, web push works only for sites added to the Home Screen, and the permission request must come from a tap ([WebKit: web push on iOS](https://webkit.org/blog/13878/web-push-for-web-apps-on-ios-and-ipados/)). Since iOS 18.4, Declarative Web Push lets the server send a simple JSON notification without complex service-worker code, which suits a solo developer ([WebKit: Declarative Web Push](https://webkit.org/blog/16535/meet-declarative-web-push/)). iOS 26 makes every Home Screen site open as a web app ([WebKit: Safari 26.0](https://webkit.org/blog/17333/webkit-features-in-safari-26-0/)). The iPhone path is still long (visit, find value, Share menu, Add to Home Screen, open, tap "Get alerts", accept), so show an instruction card only after someone taps a bell, and offer an email digest as a fallback. Google's guidance is to show your own explanation first and the browser prompt second, never on landing ([web.dev push permission UX](https://web.dev/articles/push-notifications-permissions-ux)).

### Replays, share cards and install turn spikes into returns

News brings crowds that leave. Flightradar24 normally gets about 5 million website visitors a day and **reached 20 million visits on 28 February 2026**, about four times normal, during strikes on Iran ([Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/2026/03/17/how-flightradar24-became-aviations-most-watched-screen-as-flights-are-disrupted)). Watch Duty became the top free app on Apple's App Store during the January 2025 Los Angeles fires ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/01/09/watch-duty-surpasses-chatgpt-as-top-free-app-on-app-store-as-california-fires-spread)). What converts a spike into return visits is saved places, alerts and things worth sharing. A free short replay ("what flew over you in the last hour", "last 24 hours of quakes and fires around you") is cheap because the feeds already include the past day; competitors sell longer history as their premium lever, so keep long archives for later. Share cards work when they are personal and comparable: Spotify Wrapped 2025 reached **more than 200 million engaged users and 500 million shares in its first 24 hours**, driven by personal stats and friend comparison ([TechCrunch on Wrapped 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/12/04/spotify-says-wrapped-2025-is-its-biggest-yet-with-200m-users-in-its-first-day)). Build three share paths: a server-made 1200x630 link card kept under about 300 to 600 KB for WhatsApp, a downloadable PNG because Instagram captions show no link previews, and later a 9:16 video clip ([WhatsApp link previews](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/link-previews/); [Mediabunny](https://mediabunny.dev/guide/introduction)). Android's in-app browsers (Instagram, Facebook) do not support the Web Share API, so add an "open in browser" nudge ([MDN browser compat data](https://github.com/mdn/browser-compat-data/blob/main/api/Navigator.json)).

Home-screen install matters mainly because installed web apps escape Chrome's auto-revoke and are the only way to get push on iPhone. No credible public benchmark for install rates exists, so measure your own ([Hacker News on PWA install stats](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=40959416)). Skip streaks: Duolingo's streak tests lifted 7-day retention by 14 percent, but users cannot control whether an aurora or quake happens, so a streak would reward empty visits ([Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-streaks-keep-duolingo-learners-committed-to-their-language-goals/)). Better later additions are a monthly "your sky" recap stored in the browser and "I saw it" or "I felt it" buttons. USGS's "Did You Feel It?" has gathered **over 5 million felt reports since 1999**, and researchers note that reporting gives people a sense of being useful ([USGS publication](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70210212)). Keep such reports moderated and visibly separate from official data; Citizen's 2021 misidentification of an arson suspect shows the downside of alarm-driven crowd reports ([Contrary Research on Citizen](https://research.contrary.com/company/citizen)).

## Per-layer live tool pages are the front doors

Because "around me" has almost no search volume, **the radar is the product and the layers are the marketing**. Each search door should be the radar itself, opened on one layer, with the live answer printed in the page's HTML, a few lines on method and sources, and the same "use my location" upgrade. One page per real intent, not per phrasing: "aurora forecast", "northern lights tonight" and "will I see the northern lights tonight" belong on one page, because Google's May 2026 guidance says that creating separate content "for every possible variation of how people might search", including fan-out queries, violates its scaled content abuse policy ([Google AI optimization guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide)).

Two more page types earn links and long-tail traffic. **Event pages** (each named storm, each felt or M5.5+ quake, each eruption, each strong geomagnetic storm, each meteor shower peak) give journalists a citable URL with data nobody else assembles, such as the track, the ShakeMap ring and a timeline. Noindex trivial events. **Place pages** are justified only where both the data and the demand differ by place: aurora chances for high-latitude regions, earthquake counts by country, typhoon tracking by country. Never build "what is around me in [city]" pages. The demand is near zero, and thousands of near-identical pages are exactly what the spam rules target. Personal share pages stay noindex.

Google's rules allow templated pages but punish pages "generated for the primary purpose of manipulating Search rankings and not helping users", and the current policy names "using generative AI tools ... to generate many pages without adding value" as an example ([Google, March 2024](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2024/03/core-update-spam-policies); [Google spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)). If you offer an embeddable widget, give it a plain brand link, ideally nofollow, because Google lists "keyword-rich, hidden, or low-quality links embedded in widgets" as link spam ([Google spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)).

## Relay warnings word for word and expect disasters to pay badly

This section summarises research, not legal advice. A lawyer who knows consumer, media and data-protection law in your home country and main markets should review the terms, privacy notice and alert wording before launch.

### Never sound like the authority

The rule that recurs everywhere is that official warnings belong to official bodies. "The NWS is the sole United States official voice for issuing warnings during life-threatening weather situations" ([NOAA JetStream](https://www.noaa.gov/jetstream/nws_intro)). NWS content is public domain, but you may not imply endorsement or "modify its content and then present it as official government material" ([NWS disclaimer](https://www.weather.gov/disclaimer)). Canada's licence requires alerts to be reproduced with "no alteration of the content or intent of the alert" ([ECCC licence](https://eccc-msc.github.io/open-data/licence/readme_en/)). WMO reaffirmed in 2025 that national services are "the single authoritative voice" on weather warnings ([WMO](https://wmo.int/media/news/ai-powered-meteorology-supports-early-warnings-all)). Japan's law says no one other than JMA may give warnings for Japan, and China's Meteorology Law bars anyone else from issuing forecasts or severe-weather warnings to the public; in July 2026 Chinese state media warned bloggers that amateur AI typhoon forecasts could break that law ([WMO compendium on Japan](https://wmo.int/sites/default/files/2024-05/Public-Private%20Engagement%20in%20Japan%20Underpinned%20by%20its%20Legislative%20Framework_Final_2020-10-16_2.pdf); [Meteorology Law of the PRC](https://www.asianlii.org/cn/legis/cen/laws/ml172/); [South China Morning Post](https://www.scmp.com/news/china/science/article/3360171/typhoon-bavi-nears-chinese-bloggers-warned-amateur-ai-forecasts-may-be-illegal)). FEMA's US alert feed is free but states "Alerts cannot be used for advertising purposes", which is ambiguous for a page that also carries ads ([FEMA IPAWS feed](https://www.fema.gov/emergency-managers/practitioners/integrated-public-alert-warning-system/technology-developers/all-hazards-information-feed)).

In practice: show official alerts verbatim, with the issuer's name, issue and expiry times and a link; never add your own severity words; and call anything you compute yourself (a quake 40 km away, an aurora chance) information, never a warning. Avoid "predict", "early warning", "safe" and "all clear". USGS states that nobody has ever predicted a major earthquake, and even Google's system sent its top-level alert to only 469 people for the first M7.8 Turkey quake in 2023, against about 10 million it estimated could have received one ([USGS FAQ](https://www.usgs.gov/faqs/can-you-predict-earthquakes); [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2025/07/28/google-android-earthquake-alerts-partial-failure-turkey-2023/)). The operational risks are bigger than the lawsuit risks. In 2018 AccuWeather pushed an NWS **test tsunami message to app users as a real warning** across the US Gulf and East coasts ([Texas Tribune](https://www.texastribune.org/2018/02/06/gulf-coast-tsunami-warning-was-false-alarm-officials-say)). So accept only live alert messages, honour cancellations and expiry, and when a feed is stale write "No alerts received from [source] as of [time]" rather than showing an empty, reassuring map. For tsunamis, show only "a message was issued by [centre]" with a link.

### Disclaimers help but do not replace careful design

Comparable products use the same building blocks. Zoom Earth, the closest analogue, says its information is "for general informational purposes only", must not be used "as a substitute for official warnings, local authorities, emergency services", and caps liability at what the user paid in the last twelve months, which is zero for a free product ([Zoom Earth terms](https://zoom.earth/legal/terms/)). US courts have been kind to forecasters: a USD 10 million suit against The Weather Channel over a missed storm was dismissed in 1999, partly because mass media owe no legal duty to the general public ([Klein 2003](https://sciencepolicy.colorado.edu/admin/publication_files/2003.31.pdf)). Two inferences from the notes deserve a lawyer's eye. EU and UK consumer law generally prevents excluding liability for death or injury caused by negligence, so blanket exclusions may not hold there. And push alerts tailored to a user's saved place look more like a service relationship than a broadcast, which could weaken the "no duty to the public" defence.

### Keep location on the phone

Location is personal data under GDPR ([GDPR Article 4](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-4-gdpr/)). Europe's data-protection board says that information produced on the device becomes subject to the ePrivacy consent rules once it is sent back over the network ([EDPB Guidelines 2/2023](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/documents/2024-10/edpb_guidelines_202302_technical_scope_art_53_eprivacydirective_v2_en_0.pdf)). US states are banning the sale of precise location: Oregon from 1 January 2026, Virginia from 1 July 2026 and Connecticut from 1 October 2026, joining Maryland ([Oregon DOJ](https://www.doj.state.or.us/consumer-protection/id-theft-data-breaches/privacy/privacy-law-faqs-for-consumers/); [Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/07/us-state-consumer-privacy-law-update-notable-changes-across-existing-frameworks)). The Weather Channel app settled a Los Angeles lawsuit in 2020 over vague location disclosures ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2020/08/20/ibm-settles-la-lawsuit-over-the-weather-channel-app-selling-user-location-data/)). The safe pattern: filter data on the device where possible, round coordinates to about 0.1 degree (roughly 11 km) before any request, never log raw coordinates, never pass location to ad or analytics tags, and say so plainly in the privacy notice. When ads arrive, Google requires a Google-certified consent platform using the IAB framework to serve personalised ads in the EEA, UK and Switzerland ([AdSense consent requirements](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/13554116?hl=en)), and the UK regulator says advertising cookies never fall under a consent exception ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guidance-on-the-use-of-storage-and-access-technologies/what-are-the-exceptions/)).

### Disaster pages are poor ad inventory

Google's AdSense policy, last updated 4 August 2026, lets Google "take a variety of actions" during a "Sensitive Event", and its advertiser rules name natural disasters as an example ([AdSense program policies](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/48182?hl=en); [Google Ads sensitive events](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16489952?hl=en)). Graphic "shocking content" gets restricted ad demand, and the advertising industry's content taxonomy has a top-level **"Disasters" category** that buyers can exclude wholesale ([Google publisher restrictions](https://support.google.com/publisherpolicies/answer/10437795?hl=en); [IAB Tech Lab taxonomies](https://github.com/InteractiveAdvertisingBureau/Taxonomies)). Keyword blocking is blunt: an Australian audit released on 27 September 2026 found **57 percent of news articles contained at least one term that could trigger an exclusion** ([Mediaweek](https://mediaweek.com.au/brand-safety-tools-exclude-one-in-two-news-articles-research-finds)). No CPM or fill-rate data for weather or disaster sites was found, so any revenue figure would be a guess. The direction is clear, though: **treat disaster spikes as reach, not revenue**. Put ads on the sky pages and the calm general radar, keep them visually away from alert panels, consider switching them off on pages about an ongoing major disaster, and never place survival-kit or generator affiliates next to live alerts. Keep a second income path in mind, because the strongest hazard brand in the research is funded by people, not ads: Watch Duty's members paid for 84 percent of its 2025 budget ([Watch Duty annual report](https://www.watchduty.org/blog/2025-annual-report)). Respectful tone is part of the same discipline. Crisis-communication guidance asks for accuracy, empathy and something useful to do, and the Red Cross code asks that disaster victims be shown as "dignified humans, not hopeless objects" ([CDC CERC](https://www.cdc.gov/cerc/media/pdfs/CERC_Introduction.pdf); [IFRC Code of Conduct](https://www.ifrc.org/sites/default/files/2021-07/code-of-conduct-movement-ngos-english.pdf)). That rules out casualty counters, "deadly" headlines and gamified disaster scores.

### Licences: your ads argument is reasonable, but provider terms decide

Your view that ads alone do not make a use commercial is reasonable but legally unsettled for Creative Commons NonCommercial data. Creative Commons says the test "turns on the use", and its interpretation page does not address advertising ([Creative Commons NonCommercial interpretation](https://wiki.creativecommons.org/wiki/NonCommercial_interpretation)). The point is mostly moot for this project, because the sources that matter set their own terms that restrict commercial, for-profit or ad-funded use directly, whatever NonCommercial means (Open-Meteo, RainViewer, WAQI, Blitzortung). Build every core layer on clean sources, keep anything doubtful optional and removable, and email the provider before ads go on. If you later wrap the site as an app, note that Apple says location APIs "shouldn't be used to provide emergency services" and Google Play bans apps that "capitalize on" natural disasters ([App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/); [Google Play policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/17517561?hl=en)), so present it as hazard information, never as an emergency service.

## Keep WordPress for pages and move live data off WP-Cron

The architecture that fits your existing hosting has four parts. **Cloudflare's free plan** sits in front of the whole domain for DNS, TLS and caching. **WordPress** on your current host serves the door pages, event pages, explainers, methodology, sources and attribution, privacy notice and terms. **The 3D radar** is a static JavaScript app in a folder such as /radar/ on the same domain. **A data fetcher** runs on a real schedule, outside WordPress, and writes compact JSON snapshots that the CDN serves to everyone. Upstream load then depends on your schedule, not on how many people visit.

**Why not WP-Cron.** This is standard WordPress behaviour rather than something the research measured. WP-Cron is not a real clock: WordPress checks for due tasks only when someone loads a page, and runs them inside that web request under PHP's time limits. With no visitors nothing runs, and once Cloudflare caches your pages, fewer requests reach WordPress, so WP-Cron fires even less reliably. Overlapping runs and host throttling make minute-level fetching worse. It is fine for daily housekeeping. If you run any fetch code inside WordPress at all, switch off its page-load trigger and call it from a real system cron.

**Why shared hosting struggles in a spike.** Also general hosting knowledge, to confirm against your plan: each uncached WordPress page view runs PHP and a database query, and shared plans cap concurrent PHP processes, CPU and memory. A share spike of thousands of simultaneous visitors can exhaust those caps (error pages) or trigger the host's resource suspension, and "unmetered bandwidth" usually comes with a fair-use clause. The protection is to make sure almost nothing reaches PHP: the app and data files are static and long-cached, and WordPress HTML for the door pages is cached at Cloudflare for one to five minutes with a cache rule (Cloudflare does not cache HTML by default). On Cloudflare, "requests to static assets are free and unlimited" on Workers static hosting, while dynamic Worker calls are capped at **100,000 a day on the free plan**, after which visitors get Error 1027 unless the route is set to "fail open" ([Cloudflare Workers pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/pricing/); [Cloudflare Workers limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/limits/)). The other free hosts fail worse in a spike: Netlify's free plan pauses the site when credits run out, and Vercel's Hobby plan is non-commercial and makes you wait 30 days after you exceed a limit ([Netlify credits](https://docs.netlify.com/manage/accounts-and-billing/billing/billing-for-credit-based-plans/how-credits-work/); [Vercel Hobby](https://vercel.com/docs/plans/hobby)).

**The fetcher has three workable homes.** Option A, if your host allows real cron jobs (cPanel cron or SSH crontab) and outbound HTTPS: a PHP, Python or Node script runs every minute or few minutes, fetches each source on its own cadence (USGS every minute, aurora every 5, FIRMS every 15, CelesTrak every 2 hours), validates it and writes JSON files into a public /data/ folder that Cloudflare caches for 30 to 60 seconds. Option B, if the host has no real cron: a Cloudflare Worker with a Cron Trigger writes snapshots to R2, whose free tier includes 10 GB and 1 million write operations a month; avoid Workers KV for this, because its free tier allows only 1,000 writes a day and a single file refreshed every 2 minutes already uses 720 ([Cloudflare Workers pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/pricing/)). The free plan gives each Worker run only 10 ms of CPU, which can be too little for parsing large feeds such as US alerts or the global fire file, and Cron Trigger limits on the free plan were not verified. Option C, a small virtual server, becomes necessary once you add raster layers that need real processing: cloud cover from ECMWF, CAMS smoke, LHASA landslides and GOES lightning files.

**Two small per-visitor endpoints** run as Workers and should fail open. One returns approximate location from Cloudflare's IP data, kept out of the cached HTML ([Cloudflare request docs](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/runtime-apis/request/)). The other returns planes near a coarse grid cell, cached for 10 to 30 seconds, so a million visitors in the same cities do not become a million adsb.lol calls. Share cards are drawn on a Worker with Satori-based tools such as workers-og and cached forever per URL ([workers-og](https://github.com/kvnang/workers-og)). Push sending comes later and needs a small database of subscriptions keyed by coarse cell and threshold.

**The front end** should be three.js with its WebGL2 renderer (r186, MIT licence, about 190 KB gzipped core) or globe.gl for a faster start (MIT, about 511 KB gzipped all-in) ([npm: three](https://registry.npmjs.org/three/latest); [globe.gl](https://github.com/vasturiano/globe.gl)). WebGL2 reaches about 96 percent of users against about 86 percent for WebGPU, and Android in-app browsers are disputed for WebGPU, so WebGL2 is the safe base ([caniuse WebGL2](https://github.com/Fyrd/caniuse/blob/main/features-json/webgl2.json); [caniuse WebGPU](https://github.com/Fyrd/caniuse/blob/main/features-json/webgpu.json)). Avoid tools billed per view: a viral month of one million Mapbox map loads would cost about $3,050 at published rates, and Cesium ion's free plan is non-commercial ([Mapbox pricing](https://www.mapbox.com/pricing); [Cesium ion pricing](https://cesium.com/platform/cesium-ion/pricing/)). Draw thousands of planes and satellites in one draw call, animate them on the GPU from a single time value, compute satellite positions in a Web Worker from OMM data, cap pixel density on phones, use glow sprites instead of full-screen bloom, and stress-test on a mid-range Android phone early, because no published benchmark exists ([deck.gl performance guide](https://deck.gl/docs/developer-guide/performance)).

## Ship "tonight above me" first, then widen the radius

The minimum lovable version is the one that always has something to show. Planes and satellites are overhead almost everywhere at every hour, the Moon and planets are always somewhere, and aurora and meteor showers give regular "tonight" reasons to come back, while hazards are usually empty near any one person. So the first release leads with the sky, keeps earthquakes as the one "under you" layer, and shows the rest of the world's hazards only as a ticker.

## Conclusion

The research turns the original framing on its head. The idea was pitched as a hazard radar with a sky on top, but the evidence says the sky should lead and the hazards should follow. The sky layers are legally clean, always populated, beautiful in 3D, untouched by Google, Apple or Microsoft, and safe for advertisers, so they carry daily habit, search entry and revenue. The hazard layers carry the moments that make people share and return, but they bring coverage holes, zone geometry, legal duties to relay words unchanged, and ad inventory that advertisers avoid. Building both on one globe is the right product. Leading with the hazards would mean fighting Google's own alert boxes for traffic that pays badly.

Being first is no longer available, since LiveEarthViewer and a wave of 2026 globes have already shipped the parts. The opening that remains is quality and focus: a phone-first radar that answers "what is above, tonight, under and around me" in one glance, alerts rarely and honestly across every hazard and country, and admits where its data is thin. Watch what LiveEarthViewer does with its /here page, whether MyRadar adds the sky, and how far Watch Duty goes in 2027. The cheapest test of the whole thesis is phase 1 itself: if the aurora, ISS and planes door pages earn impressions and people share their "above me right now" cards, the hazard phases are worth the extra legal and engineering weight; if not, you will have learned it before taking on that weight.
